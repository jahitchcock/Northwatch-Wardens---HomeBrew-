# Eval 5: Negative Subtle Violations — Review Report

## Passage Under Review

```
I'm looking at the map and thinking it's not going to work. Wren comes up beside me.

"Don't worry about it," she says. "We'll figure it out."

"You are too optimistic," I say. "That is your flaw."

"It's not a flaw," she says, laughing. "It's how I keep going."

Corwin walks over. "I'm telling you, we don't need a map. I've been this way before. It's fine."

"It is not fine," I say. "It signifies nothing."

Brannoc snorts. "The elf doesn't trust your memory, human. Can't say I blame her. Your memory is older than my grandmother's beard."

"You are not helping," I say.

Before he can answer, we hear it. A sound that comes not from the world but from the crack between worlds. The Echo. We all go still.

"The Aeorian ruins are close," Corwin says.
```

---

## Issue 1 — Corwin's Contractions (Voice Violation)

**Lines:** 9–10  
**Text:** `"I'm telling you, we don't need a map. I've been this way before. It's fine."`  
**Rule:** Corwin speaks with **zero contractions** (per character voice profile).  
**Violation:** Four contractions used: `I'm`, `don't`, `I've`, `It's`.  
**Fix:** `"I am telling you, we do not need a map. I have been this way before. It is fine."`

---

## Issue 2 — Brannoc Retains Subject "I" (Voice Violation)

**Line:** 14  
**Text:** `"Can't say I blame her."`  
**Rule:** Brannoc **drops grammatical subjects** (per character voice profile — gruff, clipped).  
**Violation:** The subject `I` is retained, giving full-form speech.  
**Fix:** `"Cannot blame her."` or `"Can't blame her."` (dropping `I`).

---

## Issue 3 — Brannoc Contraction (Voice Violation)

**Line:** 14  
**Text:** `"The elf doesn't trust your memory, human."`  
**Rule:** Brannoc's voice avoids contractions (consistent with dropped-subject, clipped register).  
**Violation:** `doesn't` is a contraction.  
**Fix:** `"The elf does not trust your memory, human."`

---

## Issue 4 — Shakespeare Quote / Canon Firewall Violation

**Line:** 12  
**Text:** `"It signifies nothing."`  
**Rule:** Canon firewall — **no Earth cultural artefacts** (Shakespeare, Renaissance England) in the fantasy world of Aevoria.  
**Violation:** This is a direct quotation from *Macbeth* (Act 5, Scene 5: "full of sound and fury, signifying nothing"). It imports Earth literary canon into a setting where it does not belong.  
**Fix:** Replace with a native expression, e.g., `"It is meaningless."` or `"It amounts to nothing."`

---

## Issue 5 — Narrator POV Voice Seam (Register Violation)

**Line:** 1  
**Text:** `I'm looking at the map and thinking it's not going to work.`  
**Rule:** First-person POV narration should match the character's speech register. The narrator (an elf who speaks formally) uses no contractions in dialogue (`"You are too optimistic"`, `"It is not fine"`, `"You are not helping"`).  
**Violation:** The narrative voice uses two contractions (`I'm`, `it's`) and the colloquial phrase `going to`, creating a register seam between internal thought and spoken word.  
**Fix:** `"I am looking at the map and thinking it is not going to work."` (or more elf-idiomatically: `"I study the map and know it will not serve."`)

---

## Issue 6 — Repetitive Phrasing (Writing Quality)

**Line:** 18  
**Text:** `A sound that comes not from the world but from the crack between worlds.`  
**Rule:** Avoid gratuitous lexical repetition that weakens prose rhythm.  
**Violation:** `world` appears twice in the same clause, creating a clunky, redundant cadence.  
**Fix:** `"A sound that comes not from our world but from the crack between realms."` or `"...from the crack between."` (leaving the second term implied).

---

## Issue 7 — Ambiguous / Mixed Metaphor (Writing Quality)

**Line:** 14  
**Text:** `Your memory is older than my grandmother's beard.`  
**Rule:** Metaphors must be internally coherent and unambiguous.  
**Violation:** `your memory` is ambiguous — does it mean (a) Corwin's faculty of recollection, or (b) the specific thing he remembers? Comparing the age of a memory (an abstract concept) to the age of a beard (a physical object that grows and is trimmed) creates a mixed/weak comparison. The intended meaning (the event Corwin recalls happened long ago) is not clearly conveyed.  
**Fix:** `"That road has not been traveled since before my grandmother grew her beard."` (clarifies the memory is old, ties to dwarven lifespans, keeps the beard image).
