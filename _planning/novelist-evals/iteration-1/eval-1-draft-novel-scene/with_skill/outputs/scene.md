We camped in the lee of a standing stone. The fire was small — we did not dare a beacon in those miles — and the dark pressed close around it.

"There was a mine," Brannoc said. "Back in Tharundor. Deep-workings, the old kind, where the stone sings if you know how to listen. My cousin Dennik could hear it better than any of us. Could feel a fault line three days before it moved."

He paused. The fire popped.

"Didn't have three days."

I waited.

"Took four shifts. When they got to him the stone had folded around him like it was keeping him. Elders said the mountain claimed what it made."

Corwin said nothing for a long moment. Then: "The mountain did not claim him. The stone chose to hold him."

Brannoc narrowed his eyes. "That is a strange thing to say, human."

"Is it."

I thought of the Nullwood then, and how we gave our dead back to the roots. How the trees took them apart slowly, season by season, until nothing remained but the memory we carried in our voices. Dwarves kept their dead close, wrapped in the earth's bones, held by the weight of the mountain. We let ours dissolve into loam and leaf. I did not know which was crueler.

*I was young. I did not yet know that every people keeps its grief in the only way it can bear.*
