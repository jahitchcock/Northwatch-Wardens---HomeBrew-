We ate in silence for a while, the fire snapping between us. I watched Emrik stare into the flames, his dwarf's hands held out to catch the warmth. The older man—Torbin—passed him the last of the bread, and Emrik took it without looking up.

Then Emrik began to speak. Not to us, exactly. To the fire.

"There was a cousin of mine. Briga. Worked the Greenwater lode, three hundred spans down. One morning the timbers groaned and she didn't make it out." He paused. "They sealed the seam with her inside. That's what we do. We don't bring them up. We bring the mountain down around them and call it a funeral."

I opened my mouth, but nothing came. I thought of our own dead—wrapped in clean linen, laid in the earth with prayers and flowers, visited for generations. We kept them close, let them soften into soil beneath our feet.

Torbin said, very quietly: "She is still held."

Emrik nodded once. "Aye. The whole mountain holds her."

I looked between them—the dwarf who buried his cousin under a mile of stone, the old man who spoke of being held as if he knew exactly what it meant. I did not know which was stranger, or which I envied more.
