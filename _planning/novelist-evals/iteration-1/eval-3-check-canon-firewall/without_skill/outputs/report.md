# Canon Review — Passage Analysis (Without Skill)

**Passage reviewed:** Provided inline.

**Reviewer's note:** The passage uses characters Corwin and Brannoc, who belong to *The Old Songs of Aevoria* novel series. The novel series is set in deep-time Aevoria — a mythic age long before the current campaign era. The following issues arise from that context.

---

## Issue 1 — Aeorian ruins (IP import + temporal conflict)

"Aeorian" is a direct import from Critical Role's Exandria setting (the flying city of Aeor). In a standard original fantasy setting, borrowing named IP geography is a flag. Within the Northwatch Wardens campaign, Aeorian ruins do exist (e.g., Salsvault) — but the novels are set in deep-time Aevoria, thousands of years before Aeor would have existed. Corwin and Brannoc cannot visit Aeorian ruins in that era.

## Issue 2 — The Calamity (IP import + temporal conflict)

Exandria-specific cataclysm. Same problem as Aeor: it does not belong in the novel's deep-time setting. The novels have their own native cataclysm (the Sundering).

## Issue 3 — Heart of Winter (campaign element in novel setting)

An Aeorian control node from the campaign's far-north lore. Corwin claims to have visited it during "Eclipse Day celebrations" — but neither the Heart of Winter nor Eclipse Day exist in the deep-time era of the novels. This is a temporal anachronism.

## Issue 4 — Eclipse Day (campaign event in novel setting)

A major campaign-wide event tied to the Aeorian Echo arc. Referencing it in the novel setting is an anachronism. The novels' pantheon and calendar use the Radiant Queen, Stonefather, etc. — not Eclipse Day.

## Issue 5 — Northwatch Wardens (campaign organization in novel setting)

The guild does not exist in the novel's deep-time era. Characters cannot reference it.

## Issue 6 — Echo-chasers (campaign phenomenon in novel setting)

Refers to investigators of the Aeorian Echo, a campaign-specific phenomenon. The novels have no Echo, no rift — their magic traditions are Spoken-memory, resonance/geomancy, tide/storm, and fire-shaping.

## Issue 7 — Waystone Inn (campaign location in novel setting)

The guild HQ in Northreach. It does not exist in the novel's setting. The novels have their own locations: Thistlebrook, Karrendeep, Greymarch, Haldren's Ford, Stone's Rest, the Hollow Choir.

## Issue 8 — Corwin's dialogue register

Corwin says **"Aye"** — a colloquialism that does not match his formal-archaic voice. Corwin never uses contractions and speaks with weight and cadence. "Aye" reads as folksy; Corwin would say "Indeed" or "Yes."
