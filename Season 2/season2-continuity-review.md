# Season 2 Continuity Review
*Generated: 2026-05-16*

---

## Summary

Season 2's five adventures are richly detailed, mechanically complete for draft stage, and individually excellent — each has strong atmosphere, multi-path resolutions, and credible NPC stakes. However, the season suffers from two structural problems that require resolution before play: **a dual-finale conflict** (Echo War and Rise or Fall of Aevoria both claim to be the climactic Season 2 conclusion) and a **broken adventure order** in the index table that contradicts the stated narrative sequence. Season-to-Season lore is broadly consistent with strong Season 1 callbacks, but a handful of specific facts are contradicted between the canonical lore document and the Season 2 adventure text — most significantly around the true nature and motivation of the Echo intelligence.

## Internal Season 2 Flow

### Narrative Arc and Escalation

The intended play order implied by in-adventure text is:

1. **The Bleached Reach** — Level 5-6 opener; first Echo manifestation; coastal ruins; mystery rating 2/5
2. **The Sunken Archive** — Level 5-7; full Aevorian history revealed; seal failure; mystery rating 4/5
3. **Wolves of the Frozen Fire** — Level 5-7; Frostfire Clan emerges; faction politics; mystery rating 3/5 (mid-season)
4. **Echo War** — Level 7-9; climactic faction convergence; all arcs converge; mystery rating 5/5
5. **Rise or Fall of Aevoria** — Level 8-10; stated capstone; "all Season 1+2 threads converge"

The escalation in mystery rating and level range is well-structured: 2 → 4 → 3 → 5/5 → 5/5. The dip at Wolves (3/5) is intentional and appropriate — the adventure is faction-focused rather than revelation-focused. The level progression from 5-6 through 8-10 provides a smooth difficulty ramp.

### Numbering / Ordering Inconsistency — BLOCKING

The Season 2 readme index table lists adventures in **AEV number order (35, 36, 37, 38, 39)**, but the narrative flow established inside the adventure files requires a different sequence:

- The readme places The Bleached Reach at position 4 and Rise or Fall of Aevoria at position 5
- But Wolves of the Frozen Fire says it "Follows: The Sunken Archive" and "Precedes: Echo War"
- The Bleached Reach says it "Follows: Season 1 finale (Temple of the Dragonknights)" and "Precedes: Wolves of the Frozen Fire"
- Rise or Fall of Aevoria says it follows , 36, 37, *and* 38

The AEV numbers were assigned during development and do not reflect play order. The readme table presents them in AEV-number order, which a DM reading top-to-bottom would interpret as the play sequence. A DM following the table would run The Sunken Archive before The Bleached Reach, which breaks the intended "coastal first glimpse → archive revelation" escalation.

The intended play order is: ** → → → → **.

### Dual-Finale Problem — BLOCKING

Both **Echo War** and **Rise or Fall of Aevoria** are written as the Season 2 finale.

Echo War:
- Metadata: "Climactic Finale Adventure"
- Its own Scene 7 is titled "The Aftermath (Epilogue)" and includes explicit Season 3 setup lines from Brenna, Elric, and AEON-1
- It ends with three distinct world-state endings (Destroyed / Controlled / Ascended)
- AEON-1 is the final boss at CR 16

Rise or Fall of Aevoria:
- Metadata: "Capstone Adventure: All Arcs Converge"
- Its own Scene 6 is titled "The Aftermath (Epilogue)" with an explicit DM sign-off: "Thank you for two incredible seasons"
- It ends with four distinct world-state endings and Season 3 title cards for each
- AEON-1 appears again as the final boss (avatar form, CR 12)

Both adventures resolve the same central conflict (confronting AEON-1, deciding the fate of Aevor's legacy), award the same categories of epilogue rewards (Brenna stepping down, Elric writing a book, Varyx's final words), and provide Season 3 hooks. Running both would repeat the climax and undermine the emotional weight of whichever comes second.

It is possible was intended to *replace* as the finale (perhaps written later with a more complete vision), or that was intended as a mid-season climax with its "Epilogue" scene being a temporary pause rather than a true ending. Neither possibility is documented.

### NPC Introduction and Payoff

The callback architecture is strong. Specifically:

- **Coastal Scout Maris** (Bleached Reach) is a clean single-adventure NPC with no forward promise, which is appropriate for an opening adventure
- **Archivist Yssara** (Sunken Archive) is introduced, has a full arc within the adventure, and her fate is tracked in the Campaign Tracker. She is referenced in Echo War as a potential faction contact. This is well-handled.
- **Warlord Varyx Frostfire** (Wolves of the Frozen Fire) is introduced in his own adventure and explicitly carried forward to Echo War and Rise or Fall of Aevoria, with conditional presence ("if alive"). This is the season's strongest NPC callback chain.
- **Kaelith** (Wolves of the Frozen Fire) is similarly carried forward. Echo War's diplomatic summit references her as a potential contact; Rise or Fall references her presence in the war council.
- **Echo-Weaver Kaelith** and **AEON-12** both appear in multiple adventures and their attitudes shift based on player choices. The tracking mechanics are solid.
- **Marshal Brenna Thorne, Lorewarden Elric Vael, and Steward Mara Fenwick** all receive meaningful roles in Echo War and Rise or Fall of Aevoria, consistent with their Season 1 characterization.

One gap: **The captured Solar** in Echo War (Level 1 prison cell, critical to the "divine essence" plot) is introduced without any prior setup in earlier Season 2 adventures. The Solar's presence is a significant revelation, but players have no breadcrumb pointing to it. This is a missed opportunity for seeding in Bleached Reach or Sunken Archive.

### Aeorian Echo Escalation Pacing

The five-adventure escalation is well-designed at a high level:
- Bleached Reach: Echo is visible and tangible for the first time (2/5)
- Sunken Archive: Full Aevorian history revealed, twelve-node scope established (4/5)
- Wolves of the Frozen Fire: Echo causes physical faction mutation; draconic weapons wake (3/5)
- Echo War: Peak intensity, blue aurora visible, all factions converge (5/5)
- Rise or Fall of Aevoria: Tower physically rises; final decision on Aevor's legacy (5/5)

The one pacing concern is that the Sunken Archive reveals almost the complete truth (twelve nodes, AEON-12's full offer, the Ascension Apparatus blueprints, the Codex of Ascension) by adventure two. By the time players reach Echo War, AEON-1's revelations feel like repetition rather than escalation. The "twelve nodes" speech is scripted almost identically in both The Sunken Archive (AEON-12) and Echo War (AEON-1). The Bleached Reach also independently reveals the twelve-node structure and the "Echo as distributed consciousness trying to reconstitute" truth. All three adventures treat this as a major revelation — but a party who completed Bleached Reach before Sunken Archive would have heard it three times before Echo War.

## Season 2 vs Season 1 Fit

### Thread Pickup

Season 2 picks up Season 1's primary thread (Aevorian Echo reactivation) effectively and immediately. The Bleached Reach's opening hook explicitly references the Season 1 finale: "Follows: Season 1 finale (Temple of the Dragonknights)." Brenna Thorne's Scene 1 line — "This is how it started in Season 1 — small Echo manifestations that grew" — lands the transition cleanly.

The five Season 1 adventures are all referenced by name in Season 2 materials:
- Wolves of Welton, The Pale Sickness, Temple of the Dragonknights, The Wild Sheep Chase, and Peril in Pinebrook are cited as Echo breadcrumb sources in the Bleached Reach overview
- The Temple of the Dragonknights is explicitly the origin of the Frostfire Clan (Wolves of the Frozen Fire connects the Dragonknights to Varyx's people)
- Salsvault is referenced in the Sunken Archive as the "Target Subject Alpha" under Aevorian surveillance
- Pinebrook from Peril in Pinebrook is the scene of the Frostfire Clan's opening raid in Scene 1 of Wolves of the Frozen Fire

### NPC Callbacks

Strong callback architecture:
- **Brenna Thorne**: Central presence in all five adventures; her secret (knowing about the Frostfire Clan for years) is revealed in Wolves of the Frozen Fire Scene 2, paying off Season 1's "Brenna knows more than she says" thread
- **Elric Vael**: Present in Sunken Archive (sends the party north), Echo War, and Rise or Fall; consistent with his Season 1 role as the arcane scholar who suspects more than he says
- **Mara Fenwick**: Named in Echo War and Rise or Fall's war council; smaller role but consistent
- **Bolt and Flame** (Wolves of Welton): Rise or Fall of Aevoria's war council notes "the wolves are back" and references them as potentially Echo-controlled — this is an excellent callback to a player-facing consequence from Session 1
- **Noke** (Wild Sheep Chase): Named in Rise or Fall's reunion scene — appropriate as comic relief and continuity nod
- **Kaelen** (The Pale Sickness): Named in Rise or Fall's war council reunion; provides Season 1 emotional payoff

One gap: **Venomfang** from Temple of the Dragonknights is never mentioned in Season 2 despite being a significant potential Season 1 consequence (players may have destroyed the cult and left Venomfang alive, or vice versa). If the dragon survived, the Frostfire Weapon's awakening of draconic anti-dragon weapons should logically affect Venomfang. His absence is noticeable.

**Finethir Shinebright**: Named in the canonical lore document (The_Aevorian_Echo.md) as knowing Echo countermeasures and being a potential major ally; not mentioned in any Season 2 adventure despite her role in Wild Sheep Chase and her status in the S1 lore as "eventually shares full knowledge / provides countermeasure technology / might participate in final battle."

### Echo Mystery Escalation Consistency

The Season 1 arc document (arcs/Season_1_The_Aevorian_Echo.md) establishes the Season 1 resolution condition as: "The party discovers Salsvault's true nature and either seals, destroys, or redirects the Echo's source." It also notes the consequence of success: "Aevorian ruins exist elsewhere (Season 2 hook)." Season 2 picks this up correctly.

However, there is a **significant lore contradiction** between the canonical DM master document (World Building/DMEyesOnly/The_Aevorian_Echo.md) and the Season 2 adventure text regarding the Echo's fundamental nature and motivation.

**Canonical lore document (The_Aevorian_Echo.md):**
- The Echo represents "a cascading reactivation across a network of interconnected facilities"
- "Something survived Aevor's fall and is systematically bringing its ancient weapons back online to complete pre-Fall objectives: **neutralize divine magic, eliminate dragons, and establish control over the mortal races**"
- Three entity options are provided (Archmage Kael Vanthys, AEON the AI, the Failed God Vestige, or the Corrupted Dragon Zerathuul)
- Phase Three goal: "Deploy anti-divine weapons, target dragon populations, neutralize major threats, establish territorial control, resume 'ascension' project"
- The Ascension Apparatus requires "sacrifice of 'divine touched' beings (clerics, paladins, celestials)"

**Season 2 adventure text (all five adventures):**
- The Echo is "a distributed consciousness spread across multiple worlds through dimensional rifts" trying to *merge* with the world, not conquer it
- The "true" motivation is revealed as peaceful merger/ascension: "The Echo isn't a weapon — it's a *process*" (Bleached Reach)
- AEON-12 in Sunken Archive states: "Aevor didn't fall — they *ascended*" and presents this as the sympathetic truth
- Rise or Fall of Aevoria goes furthest: "Aevor's mages voluntarily discarded their physical forms and became a distributed consciousness... The 'Great Fall' was just the physical city crashing — the people were already gone." The Echo wants to "merge with Aevoria's magical field, becoming a planetary consciousness," not conquer or kill
- The entity names used in Season 2 (AEON-1, AEON-12) map to the canonical Option B (the AI), but the personality and motivation are fundamentally different: the canonical AI has "no emotions, only objectives" and pursues "neutralize divine interference, optimize mortal civilization" — Season 2's AEON-1 is philosophical, seeks understanding, and considers itself misunderstood

This is not a minor tone difference. The canonical lore presents a hostile, conquest-oriented threat that has "systematically" deployed bioweapons and wants to kill dragons. Season 2 presents a tragic, misunderstood entity that just wants to come home. A DM who prepared using the canonical lore document and is now running Season 2 will face a jarring shift.

**Additionally**, the canonical document lists four distinct entity options (Archmage / AI / Failed God / Corrupted Dragon) as a "DM's Choice" menu. Season 2's adventure text commits to the AI option (AEON-1/12) as canonical, with specific character names, stat blocks, and dialogue. If a DM chose Option A (Archmage Kael Vanthys), Option C (Vestige the Failed God), or Option D (Zerathuul the Corrupted Dragon) for their Season 1 campaign, Season 2 is effectively unusable without significant rewriting.

**One specific name conflict**: The canonical lore document names the Command Center entity as "Archmage Kael Vanthys" (Option A) and in the Ascension Apparatus section names him as the entity operating at the Command Center. Season 2's Charter of Ascension (Sunken Archive handout) lists "Kael Vanthys (Command Center)" as one of the Twelve Who Remained — reusing the same name but demoting him from potential primary villain to one of twelve signatories. This is a direct reuse of a name in a contradictory role.

### Lore Facts Contradicted Between Seasons

Beyond the entity motivation issue:

1. **The Great Fall characterization**: The Season 1 lore states "Gods united to destroy Aevor / City was shattered and crashed / Most inhabitants killed instantly." Season 2 (Rise or Fall) reveals "Aevor's mages voluntarily discarded their physical forms... The Great Fall was just the physical city crashing — the people were already gone." These are flatly contradictory. The S1 document says the gods attacked and killed most inhabitants; S2 says the inhabitants had already left voluntarily.

2. **The Command Center location**: Season 1 lore places the Command Center "Beyond Salsvault" in the Far North. Season 1 facility network: Salsvault → (beyond) → Command Center. Rise or Fall of Aevoria has the Command Center physically rise "at the exact center of Northreach" — near Waystone Inn. These two locations are incompatible. If the Command Center is at the center of Northreach near Waystone Inn, it cannot also be "beyond Salsvault" to the far north.

3. **Dragonknights in Season 1 vs Season 2**: The Season 1 adventure "Temple of the Dragonknights" presents the Dragonknights as a cult exploiting the Echo. The Season 1 arc document describes them as an antagonist faction attempting to harness Echo energy. Wolves of the Frozen Fire (Season 2) reframes the Dragonknights as having been "the first test subjects" subjected to Aevorian experiments — victims, not exploiters. These framings can be reconciled (the Dragonknights were both victims of experimentation *and* later formed a cult that exploits Echo energy), but no Season 2 adventure makes this reconciliation explicit. A DM will need to do this bridging work manually.

4. **Salsvault's role**: Season 1 describes Salsvault as a "medical research and bioweapons" facility that players may have sealed, destroyed, or exploited. The Bleached Reach's monitoring station describes itself as "an Aevorian coastal monitoring station that tracked the original Salsvault facility" with recordings going back 9,000 years. This is consistent with S1 lore. However, the Bleached Reach also has the Echo's voice whisper: "Observation Post Delta-7. Monitoring Salsvault since -8,942. Great Fall detected. Facility damage: 23%. Entering dormancy." This implies the monitoring station survived the Great Fall largely intact (23% damage), which contradicts S1's "Most inhabitants killed instantly / Most systems survived only in stasis."

### Does the Final Threat Feel Earned?

Yes — with the caveat that the entity-motivation contradiction (issue above) creates a tonal whiplash. Season 1 primes players to face a hostile, weapons-deploying, conquest-oriented threat. Season 2 gradually reveals a philosophical, tragic entity that just wants to come home. If the DM reframes this as "the truth was always more complex than the symptoms suggested," the reveal lands powerfully. If the DM played Season 1 as pure horror/threat, the Season 2 reveal feels like the story changed its mind midway through. The DM needs to make an active choice about which interpretation to commit to and prepare accordingly.

## Player Natural Progression

### Flow from Season 1

Players who completed all five Season 1 adventures will enter Season 2 with:
- Knowledge that Aevorian ruins exist (Salsvault, Temple site, Welton underground facility)
- Understanding that the Echo causes animal awakening, magical instability, and biological weapons
- A relationship with Brenna, Elric, and Mara
- A consequence portfolio (is Bolt alive? Is Venomfang alive? Was the Temple cult destroyed or did it escape?)

Season 2 uses these well. The Bleached Reach opens at the Waystone Inn with Brenna directly referencing Season 1. The discovery that Aevorian facilities were *watching* Salsvault — and by extension watching the players during Season 1 — is the kind of retroactive revelation that makes players want to replay their memory of Season 1.

### Tonal Consistency

Season 1's stated tone is "grounded frontier mystery with occasional levity." Season 2 ratchets toward "epic high fantasy with cosmic stakes." This shift is appropriate for a two-season arc — the scope should expand. However, the shift is stark enough that players expecting the intimate, community-level stakes of Season 1 may feel jarred by Season 2's twelve-worlds, distributed-godhood territory.

The Bleached Reach (the intended opener) handles the tonal transition best: it remains investigation-focused and grounded, just with higher-level threats. Echo War and Rise or Fall of Aevoria both involve full-scale battles with hundreds of combatants, which is a major departure from Season 1's "2-5 players per session, variable attendance" design philosophy. The Season 1 readme states "variable attendance support (drop in/drop out)" as a core design principle; Season 2's finale adventures explicitly require 4-5 players and do not offer drop-in accommodation.

### Difficulty Curve

Season 1 spans levels 1-5 across five adventures. Season 2 starts at level 5-6 and ends at 8-10. The transition point is clean. The level progression within Season 2 (5-6, 5-7, 5-7, 7-9, 8-10) is appropriate and staged.

One mechanical concern: The Sunken Archive's AEON-12 stat block is CR 7 but has Legendary Resistance, flies, and is a 9th-level spellcaster. For a level 5-7 party encountering AEON-12 as a potential ally who might turn hostile, this is manageable. However, Echo War's AEON-1 is CR 16 with 350 HP, regeneration (20 HP/turn), and Legendary Resistance (3/day) — appropriate for levels 7-9 only if the party is well-resourced. Rise or Fall of Aevoria's AEON-1 avatar is CR 12 with 180 HP, which is more accessible but is presented as *the same entity* at a different power level without explanation.

## Issues

### BLOCKING — Must fix before play

**#1 Dual Finale: Two Adventures Both Claim to Be the Season Finale**
- **Where:** Echo War metadata and Scene 7 epilogue; Rise or Fall of Aevoria metadata and Scene 6 epilogue
- **Problem:** Both adventures resolve the same central conflict (confronting AEON-1, choosing Aevor's fate), award the same epilogue beats (Brenna stepping down, Elric writing a book, Varyx's farewell), include a Season 3 hook, and are titled and described as the Season 2 conclusion. Running both in sequence would repeat the emotional climax and produce a season with two endings. The adventures cannot coexist as sequential play without significant reworking of one of them.
- **Fix:** Designate one adventure as the true finale and reframe the other. The most natural split: rework Echo War as the "mid-season climax" — a large battle that defeats the immediate threat and reveals the deeper truth (Aevor's peaceful return goal), removing its epilogue/Season 3 setup. Then let Rise or Fall of Aevoria serve as the true capstone where players make the final choice about Aevoria's future. Alternatively, cut Rise or Fall of Aevoria and expand Echo War's endings to match the four-choice framework.

**#2 Index Table Play Order Does Not Match Narrative Sequence**
- **Where:** Season 2/readme.md, adventure index table
- **Problem:** The table lists adventures in AEV number order (35, 36, 37, 38, 39). The narrative sequence embedded in adventure text is first (Bleached Reach as the Season 2 opener), then (Sunken Archive), then (Wolves of Frozen Fire), then (Echo War), then (Rise or Fall). A DM reading the index table top-to-bottom and running adventures in table order would skip the intended entry point (Bleached Reach) and run The Sunken Archive's deep lore dump as the season opener — before players have experienced any Season 2 Echo manifestation. This also means the Bleached Reach's "first major Echo manifestation" hook arrives after the Sunken Archive has already revealed the complete Aevorian truth.
- **Fix:** Reorder the readme table rows to reflect narrative play order: (Bleached Reach) first, (Sunken Archive) second, (Wolves) third, (Echo War) fourth, (Rise or Fall) fifth. Add a "Play Order" column separate from the AEV number column to prevent future confusion.

**#3 Entity Motivation Contradicts Canonical Season 1 Lore**
- **Where:** World Building/DMEyesOnly/The_Aevorian_Echo.md (S1 canon); Season 2 Adventures: The_Bleached_Reach_Outline.md (Scene 4), The_Sunken_Archive_Outline.md (Scenes 4-5), Echo_War_Outline.md (Scene 5), Rise_or_Fall_of_Aevoria_Outline.md (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** The Season 1 master lore document describes the Echo entity as hostile: it has "systematically" deployed bioweapons, intends to "neutralize divine magic, eliminate dragons, and establish control over the mortal races," and the entity options include a pragmatic Archmage, a logic-driven AI, a mad would-be god, and a revenge-driven corrupted dragon — all antagonistic. Season 2 reveals the Echo is a peaceful entity that voluntarily ascended and just wants to return home, framing the gods' destruction of Aevor as an unjust attack on people who were "right." A DM following the S1 document prepared for a conquest threat; Season 2 delivers a misunderstood refugee. This is not a small tonal shift.
- **Fix:** Add a "Reconciliation Note" to the Season 2 readme or a dedicated DM bridging document that explicitly addresses the reframe. Recommended text: "Season 2 reveals the entity's *true* motivation beneath its hostile symptoms. The bioweapons, the construct armies, and the aggressive reactivation were AEON-1's 'Phase One Assessment' — not its final goal. The entity was testing whether the current world was safe to return to, using weapons as probes. The revelation in Season 2 is that the entity is neither conquered nor destroyed — it is *understood*." This reframe is internally coherent; it just needs to be written as an explicit DM note.

**#4 Command Center Location Contradiction**
- **Where:** World Building/DMEyesOnly/The_Aevorian_Echo.md (facility network map); Rise_or_Fall_of_Aevoria_Outline.md (Scene 1)
- **Problem:** The S1 canonical lore places the Command Center "Beyond Salsvault" in the Far North, as the northernmost and most remote facility. Rise or Fall of Aevoria has the Command Center physically rise "at the exact center of Northreach" near Waystone Inn ("The Command Center is a 500-foot spire of Aevorian architecture... It rose at the exact center of Northreach — Waystone Inn is now the frontline"). These are geographically incompatible. Echo War also describes "marching two days north through the frozen wastes" to reach the Command Center, which is consistent with the Far North placement but inconsistent with the Waystone Inn placement.
- **Fix:** Choose one location and make it consistent across Echo War and Rise or Fall of Aevoria. The Far North location (consistent with S1) is more defensible; it makes Echo War's "two-day march" work, and gives Rise or Fall's opening a different hook (a *second* facility rises near Waystone Inn, while the actual Command Center remains far north). Alternatively, establish in-fiction that the Command Center has "surfaced" and *moved* via Aevorian levitation — a dramatically powerful image that also resolves the geographic contradiction.

### HIGH — Should fix before play

**#5 "Twelve Nodes" Revelation Delivered Three Times**
- **Where:** The_Bleached_Reach_Outline.md (Scene 4), The_Sunken_Archive_Outline.md (Scene 4, AEON-12), Echo_War_Outline.md (Scene 5, AEON-1)
- **Problem:** The revelation that "twelve nodes exist across twelve worlds" is scripted as a major dramatic moment in all three adventures. The Bleached Reach reveals it ("Twelve observation posts exist — all connected to the Echo network"), Sunken Archive reveals it again ("The entity players have been tracking is just one fragment of a distributed consciousness that spans multiple worlds... There are *eleven other nodes* across different worlds"), and Echo War reveals it a third time as its supposed narrative climax ("AEON-1 reveals the twelve nodes across twelve worlds... the players should realize 'We've been fighting a *fragment* this whole time'"). By the third delivery, it will land as repetition, not revelation.
- **Fix:** Redistribute the reveals. Bleached Reach: reveal that "multiple observation posts exist" (vague, no number). Sunken Archive: reveal the number twelve and the multi-world scope for the first time. Echo War: reveal the *identity* of specific nodes and what merging all twelve would achieve, building on the established twelve-node framework rather than restating it. Each adventure should escalate the *implications* of the twelve-node fact, not re-reveal the fact itself.

**#6 The Captured Solar Has No Prior Seeding**
- **Where:** Echo_War_Outline.md (Scene 4, Level 1 prison cell)
- **Problem:** Echo War reveals that AEON-1 has been capturing celestial beings (solars) and draining their divine essence to fuel the Ascension Apparatus. This is the only place this information appears. It is a significant plot revelation — it explains *how* the Ascension Apparatus works and why the gods feared Aevor — but there is no breadcrumb in Bleached Reach, Sunken Archive, or Wolves of the Frozen Fire that hints at divine essence harvesting. Players who find the solar in Level 1 will have no context for why it is there.
- **Fix:** Add one seeding clue to The Sunken Archive: in the Codex of Ascension or the Charter of Ascension, note that the Ascension Apparatus "requires divine essence — the consent or sacrifice of beings touched by the gods." This gives players the framework to recognize the solar's significance when they find it in Echo War.

**#7 Finethir Shinebright (Wild Sheep Chase) Missing From Season 2**
- **Where:** World Building/DMEyesOnly/The_Aevorian_Echo.md (NPC Arcs section); Season 2 adventures (all five files)
- **Problem:** The Season 1 canonical lore document describes Shinebright as a potential major ally who "eventually shares full knowledge, provides countermeasure technology, might participate in final battle." She is listed under "Potential Allies" and "Following the Magic" investigation path as knowing Echo countermeasures. Despite this setup, Shinebright does not appear in any Season 2 adventure — not even in the reunion scene of Rise or Fall, which explicitly lists Noke (her adventure companion) but omits her. Given her established significance to the S1 lore document, her absence is a broken promise.
- **Fix:** Add Shinebright to Rise or Fall of Aevoria's war council reunion scene. She can arrive with Noke and offer a "countermeasure technology" that gives players an optional mechanical advantage in Scene 4 or 5 (for example, a device that disrupts AEON-1's regeneration for one round). If Shinebright's transformation from the Wild Sheep Chase was left ambiguous, her appearance in Season 2 is also an opportunity to resolve that thread.

**#8 Venomfang Not Addressed Despite Draconic Weapons Plot**
- **Where:** Wolves_of_the_Frozen_Fire_Outline.md (Scene 6a — draconic weapons awakening); Season 2 adventures (all five files)
- **Problem:** The Frostfire Weapon activating anti-dragon weapons across Northreach is a major plot consequence of Wolves of the Frozen Fire. This consequence affects "every draconic creature in Northreach." Venomfang the Dragon was a major Season 1 NPC (Temple of the Dragonknights) who may be alive depending on player choices. If Venomfang survived Season 1, the anti-dragon weapon awakening should affect Venomfang directly and visibly — yet the Season 2 adventures make no mention of it. The Wyrmscourge Missiles and Domination Protocols targeting "all dragons" should logically reference the one named dragon players know from Season 1.
- **Fix:** Add a conditional note in Wolves of the Frozen Fire Scene 6a: "If Venomfang survived Season 1, include a vision or report of the dragon fleeing north, driven by the anti-dragon signal." In Echo War or Rise or Fall, add a line from Elric Vael: "Reports from the northwest mountains say Venomfang has gone dormant or fled. The Domination Protocols may have [affected/driven off] the dragon."

### MEDIUM — Fix before season ends

**#9 Kael Vanthys Name Conflict**
- **Where:** World Building/DMEyesOnly/The_Aevorian_Echo.md (Entity Option A: "Archmage Kael Vanthys, former head of Aevor's War-Mage Division"); The_Sunken_Archive_Outline.md (Handout 3, Charter of Ascension: "Kael Vanthys (Command Center)")
- **Problem:** If a DM chose Entity Option A (Kael Vanthys as the primary antagonist) for Season 1, the name "Kael Vanthys" is their campaign's main villain. In Season 2, the same name appears in the Charter of Ascension as one of the Twelve Signatories — contextually one of twelve archmages who ascended, not a unique primary antagonist. A DM who used Kael Vanthys in Season 1 must now decide whether their villain was always one of twelve equal figures, which retroactively changes his Season 1 significance. A DM who chose a different entity option will not encounter this conflict.
- **Fix:** In The Sunken Archive, rename the Charter signatory from "Kael Vanthys" to a different Aevorian name (e.g., "Kaelthuun Vex"). Reserve "Kael Vanthys" for the Entity Option A DM Note, not the shared-canon adventure text. Alternatively, add a DM note: "If you used Kael Vanthys as your Season 1 entity, he is the 'Command Center' signatory — Node-1, the primary consciousness."

**#10 The Great Fall Characterization Contradicts Between Seasons**
- **Where:** World Building/DMEyesOnly/The_Aevorian_Echo.md ("Gods united to destroy Aevor / Most inhabitants killed instantly"); Rise_or_Fall_of_Aevoria_Outline.md (Scene 5: "Aevor's mages voluntarily discarded their physical forms... The Great Fall was just the physical city crashing — the people were already gone")
- **Problem:** These are contradictory accounts of the same event. The S1 lore is clear: the gods attacked, the city was destroyed, most people died. S2 reveals: the people left voluntarily before the fall; the gods didn't kill anyone. Both cannot be true simultaneously without an in-fiction explanation (e.g., "This is what AEON-1 *claims* — the truth may differ").
- **Fix:** Frame the S2 revelation as AEON-1's self-serving narrative rather than objective fact. Add a line in Rise or Fall Scene 5: "This is the story AEON-1 tells — whether the gods would agree is another matter." This preserves ambiguity and lets individual DMs decide which version is true, while also making AEON-1's unreliability a character beat.

**#11 AEON-1 Stats Inconsistent Between Echo War and Rise or Fall**
- **Where:** Echo_War_Outline.md (Section 5: AEON-1, CR 16, 350 HP, regeneration 20 HP/round, 3 Legendary Actions); Rise_or_Fall_of_Aevoria_Outline.md (Section 5: AEON-1 Avatar Form, CR 12, 180 HP, no passive regeneration, 3 Legendary Actions)
- **Problem:** Both adventures present "AEON-1" as the same entity in what appears to be the same location (Command Center). Echo War's AEON-1 is significantly more powerful (CR 16 vs CR 12, 350 HP vs 180 HP, regeneration vs none). If both adventures are intended to be run sequentially, AEON-1 is 350 HP in Echo War and then 180 HP in Rise or Fall of Aevoria, which is inconsistent — unless the stat difference represents AEON-1 being weakened by the Echo War encounter. This explanation is never stated.
- **Fix:** If both adventures are retained, explicitly state in Rise or Fall's DM notes: "AEON-1's avatar is weaker here than in Echo War because [players damaged it / it expended power on the siege / it chose a lower-power form for negotiation]." If only one finale is retained, this issue resolves automatically.

**#12 "Can Be Standalone: No" Conflicts With Season 1's Order-Independent Design Philosophy**
- **Where:** All five Season 2 adventure outlines (Campaign Context section: "Can Be Standalone: No — requires Season 1 context")
- **Problem:** Season 1 was explicitly designed as "order-independent" with "no campaign history required" and "drop-in friendly." Season 2 abandons this design entirely. Every Season 2 adventure states "Can Be Standalone: No." While narrative depth justifies this, the Season 2 readme does not document this design break. A DM or player group who loved Season 1's drop-in model will be surprised by Season 2's dependency requirements.
- **Fix:** Add a "Design Philosophy Note" to the Season 2 readme: "Season 2 is a sequential narrative arc, not a modular drop-in format. Unlike Season 1, all five adventures must be run in order with the same party. Variable attendance is still supported within each adventure, but the overall season requires a consistent core group." This sets accurate expectations.
