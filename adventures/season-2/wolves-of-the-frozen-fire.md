## Quick Brief

Wolves of the Frozen Fire is Season 2's faction adventure — the one where the players discover the Echo isn't just an abstract threat but has been doing things to *people* for a thousand years. The Frostfire Clan, breakaway Dragonknights who vanished after the Great Fall, emerges from the northern wastes with blue fire that burns without heat. They're raiding settlements. But they're not villains — they're a community of draconic-human hybrids caught between Aevor's experiments and the Echo's reactivation, trying to protect their children. Warlord Varyx Frostfire is tired, ancient, and desperate, not evil. The adventure escalates from Season 1 by revealing that the Temple of the Dragonknights had survivors — and those survivors have been waiting, changing, and building toward this confrontation for a millennium. Tone: cold, isolated, morally heavy. Combat is available but negotiation is the better path.

## Key Scenes

### Scene 1: The Raid on Pinebrook

**Location:** Pinebrook — the settlement from Season 1's "Peril in Pinebrook," nighttime blizzard  
**Setup:** This is a callback and a combat encounter simultaneously. The frostfire is visually striking — blue flames that cast cold light. Frostfire warriors deal cold + fire damage simultaneously (resistance to one doesn't prevent the other). The raid is surgical, not murderous — they're searching for something specific.

**What happens:**
- 6 Frostfire Warriors + 1 Frostfire Shaman raid in coordinated patterns
- Their target is the town hall basement — specifically "The Dragon Scrolls" stored there from Season 1 cleanup
- 30+ civilians need protection — players can prioritize combat or rescue (both are valid)
- Raiders retreat when they have what they came for, regardless of combat state
- They leave behind the Frostfire Manifesto — a parchment in Draconic demanding return of "the Temple and all Aevorian artifacts"
- If players examine wounded raiders: draconic features (scales on neck, slit pupils), armor bearing the corrupted Dragonknight sigil

**Key rolls:**
- DC 12 Investigation on raiders' armor: corrupted Dragonknight symbol with blue flames overlaid
- DC 14 Medicine on wounded raider: draconic physical modifications — not cosmetic, deeply biological
- DC 16 Arcana on frostfire: Aevorian in origin; feels like Echo magic
- DC 13 Investigation in town hall after: they were searching for "The Dragon Scrolls"
- DC 15 Perception in town hall: the Manifesto left behind; a hidden compartment forced open
- DC 18 Investigation in town hall: the compartment held something that's now gone — a small Aevorian device

**Time pressure:** The blizzard reduces visibility to 60 feet (DC 12 Perception). If players focus only on combat and ignore civilians, 1d4 civilians take serious injuries. Frostfire Shaman casts *darkness* and *hold person* to control the battlefield.

**Escalation:** If players are losing the combat, Brenna Thorne arrives with 4 Warden guards (she was expecting trouble). If players have already secured most civilians, the Shaman withdraws and the remaining warriors follow as a coordinated retreat.

### Scene 2: The Frostfire Manifesto

**Location:** Waystone Inn, private meeting room, morning after the raid  
**Setup:** Brenna unfurls the blood-stained parchment. This is an information scene — players piece together who the Frostfire Clan is, what they want, and why Brenna knew about them.

**What happens:**
- Brenna's revelation: she's known about the Frostfire Clan for years; hoped they'd stay in the north; they've been watching since Season 1
- The Manifesto reveals: they were Dragonknights who didn't surrender after the Great Fall; Aevor subjected them to "Project: Frostfire"; they've survived for centuries in the Far North, changed by the experiments
- The Echo's reactivation is awakening their latent draconic modifications — they're becoming *more* draconic
- Key question the players can ask Brenna: *"Why didn't you tell us?"* Her answer: *"Because I hoped they'd stay buried. Some secrets are better left in the ice. But the Echo is waking everything up."*
- Brenna provides: map to Ember Crag, Dragonknight Journal (found in Pinebrook during investigation), and her assessment that the Wardens (40 strong) cannot fight the Clan (200+ enhanced) head-on

**Key rolls:**
- DC 14 Intelligence/Aevorian (to read Manifesto without help): quotes about Dragonknight betrayal, draconic flesh bonded to cold fire, the "birthright" Aevor promised
- DC 16 History (research): Dragonknights were founded to fight Aevor's draconic weapons; some were captured during the Great Fall; subjected to "Project: Frostfire"; survived for centuries
- DC 14 Persuasion (to get Brenna to share more): she'll admit she's been tracking their movements and chose not to act; this is a real moral weight for her

**Time pressure:** Brenna warns: "Every day you wait, they grow stronger. The Echo is feeding their power. In a week, they'll be unstoppable."

{{note
##### Read Aloud
*"Brenna Thorne unrolls a blood-stained parchment — the Frostfire Manifesto, left behind in the raid. Her face is pale. 'I hoped this day wouldn't come. The Frostfire Clan... they were the Dragonknights who refused to surrender after the Great Fall. Aevor changed them. And now the Echo is waking them up.' She looks at you, grim. 'We need to understand what they've become. Before more towns burn.'"*
}}

**Escalation:** If players want to mobilize a military response: Brenna shuts this down immediately. The numbers don't work. This must be surgical.

### Scene 3: The Tundra Approach

**Location:** 3 days of frozen tundra north of Northreach, approaching Ember Crag  
**Setup:** Travel montage with environmental details and a potential combat encounter. The frostfire markers along the path are worth investigating — they're not decorative. The Ember Crag comes into view on the last day of travel.

**What happens:**
- Frostfire markers line the path — Aevorian waypoints that count down miles to the Crag
- Echo-Weaver scouts (2) observe the party from a ridge; if spotted and caught, they don't fight: *"The Warlord expects you. Come to the Crag. He will... speak to you."*
- Frost-wyvern patrol (if players are reckless or the scouts alert the Crag): 2 Frost-Wyverns + 2 Frostfire Riders
- Arrival: Kaelith meets them at the base of Ember Crag — a figure in robes that seem to shift between ice and fire patterns, eyes glowing faintly blue

**Key rolls:**
- DC 12 Con save each day of travel (cold weather gear negates this entirely)
- DC 14 Survival (tracking raiders): they were mounted, fast, carrying the Dragon Scrolls
- DC 13 Arcana on frostfire markers: Aevorian waypoints, part of the facility network
- DC 12 Perception: the two Echo-Weaver scouts on the ridge; if missed, they alert Ember Crag (the Clan is fully ready for the party's arrival)

**Time pressure:** No hard pressure; reaching the Crag is the objective. Optional Frost-Wyvern combat if scouts alert.

{{note

**Escalation:** If players attack the scouts: Kaelith still emerges, but the greeting is cold. Varyx is watching via the Crag's own observation systems. He knows exactly how the players handled their last free choice before entering his home.

### Scene 4: Warlord Varyx Frostfire

**Location:** Great Hall of the Ember Crag, massive cavern carved from black ice  
**Setup:** This is the emotional core of the adventure. Varyx is not a throne-room villain — he's sitting on a throne of dragon bone, yes, but he looks *tired*. Ancient. The cavern is a community: children with scales and small wings, elders cooking at fires, murals of history carved into the walls. The Frostfire Weapon (a ring of Aevorian metal, 30 feet across) sits dormant in the center of the hall.

**What happens:**
- Varyx's opening: *"Dragonknights. Wardens. The names change, but the betrayal remains the same. You stole our Temple. You stole our birthright. Now... you'll watch us finish what Aevor started."*
- Key conversation beats: Varyx reveals they were the first test subjects; the Temple was their home after the Great Fall; the Wardens' Season 1 investigation broke their seals and woke the Echo
- The Clan is a community — children born with draconic features, elders who remember a world before the Echo's awakening
- The Frostfire Weapon is fully visible in the hall — players can examine it while talking
- If players mention Brenna: Varyx's bitterness deepens — *"Brenna knew. She's been a coward."*
- Varyx's offer: three paths (see Decision Points)
- If players attack: Varyx (reskinned Young Red Dragon, frostfire flavored; 180 HP, Frostfire Breath recharge 5-6 deals 8d6 cold + 8d6 fire half on save) with 4 Frostfire Guards and 2 Echo-Weavers

**Key rolls:**
- DC 14 Perception (observing the Clan): ~200 members, all draconic-modified; children, elders, a real community
- DC 16 Investigation on the Great Hall walls: murals show Dragonknights → Capture → Experiments → Frostfire Clan history
- DC 16 Arcana on the Frostfire Weapon: it's broadcasting a signal to the Temple site and other facilities; drawing power from the Echo network

**Time pressure:** None immediately, but Varyx won't negotiate forever. If players are evasive or dismissive without committing, he ends the meeting and gives them 24 hours to decide.

{{note

Ancient. He gestures, and the frostfire dims. 'Dragonknights. Wardens. The names change, but the betrayal remains the same. You stole our Temple. You stole our birthright. **Escalation:** If negotiations completely break down (players insulting, threatening, or refusing all three options), Varyx activates the Frostfire Weapon to 30% power to demonstrate what happens if they interfere. Move to Scene 5a or 5b based on what players do next.

### Scene 5: The Frostfire Weapon

**Location:** Frostfire Weapon Chamber (center of the Great Hall, now isolated as the ring activates)  
**Setup:** This scene splits depending on Scene 4's outcome.

**Branch A — Players negotiated or joined:** Varyx demonstrates the Weapon. Players witness his transformation — he grows more draconic, more terrible — and then realizes the Weapon has also woken Aevor's anti-dragon weapons. The situation is worse than Varyx intended.

**Branch B — Players fought and won:** The Weapon is still running at 60% and climbing (1% per round). Kaelith rushes in: *"If it reaches 100%, it broadcasts across the entire region. Every draconic creature will... change."* Players must disable or destroy it.

**What happens in Branch A:**
- Varyx transforms: *"You see? The power... it's magnificent. My people can be protected."*
- Then a cold mechanical voice cuts through: *"Draconic test subjects: Enhanced. Commencing Phase Two: Weapon Deployment."*
- Varyx's eyes go wide: *"No... that's not... that wasn't supposed to happen."*
- Phase Two is Aevor's anti-dragon weapons activating — the Clan is now a *target*, not an asset
- Players must choose: help Varyx reverse it, or let Phase Two complete

**What happens in Branch B (sabotage):**
- DC 18 Strength/Thieves' Tools: Destroy the control crystal (fast, dangerous, 4d6 lightning to nearby on failure)
- DC 16 Arcana: Safely disable via Aevorian code rewrite (slower, safer)
- DC 15 Investigation: Find the manual override hidden panel
- 6 Frostfire Warriors defend the Weapon fanatically; 2 Echo-Weavers try to complete activation

**Key rolls (Branch A):**
- DC 16 Arcana: The Weapon is also waking up Aevor's anti-dragon weapons stored at the Temple site
- DC 16 Persuasion: Convince Varyx to help reverse it (he knows the shutdown code)
- DC 14 Medicine (saving the Clan's children): stabilize the young who are changing rapidly; prevents complete draconic transformation

**Time pressure (Branch B):** The Weapon hits 100% power after 40 rounds (about 4 minutes combat time). At 100%, every draconic creature in Northreach gets the amplification signal.

## Decision Points

- **Scene 4 — Varyx's offer:**  
  *If they join/help activate the Weapon:* Frostfire Clan becomes a potential ally; anti-dragon weapons activate; Aevor's Phase Two begins; more dangerous run-up to Echo War.  
  *If they fight and sabotage the Weapon:* Clan is safe but vulnerable and leaderless; anti-dragon weapons still activated by ambient Echo; Varyx is grateful/bitter; players may recruit the Clan.  
  *If they fight and Varyx dies:* Clan scatters violently; some become bandits; harder Echo War with no Frostfire allies.

- **Scene 5 (Branch A) — help Varyx reverse Phase Two vs. let it complete:**  
  *If they help Varyx:* Anti-dragon weapons activate but Clan is not destroyed; Varyx accepts their help (shifts toward ally); Aevor's weapons are now the region's problem.  
  *If they let Phase Two complete:* Every draconic creature in Northreach is amplified AND targeted; Frostfire children transform into beasts; Varyx is broken; worst regional outcome.

- **Aftermath — what to do with the Clan:**  
  *Recruit them:* DC 16 Persuasion; 50+ enhanced soldiers for Echo War; Varyx is a powerful if complicated ally.  
  *Help them relocate:* Diplomatic success; peaceful neighbor; no soldiers for the war.  
  *Disband them:* No Frostfire Clan in Echo War; easier path but no allied force.

## Hooks Forward

- Anti-dragon weapons activated by the Frostfire Weapon (regardless of Branch A or B) are now awake across Northreach — this is one of Echo War's major complications.
- Kaelith, if she survives as an ally, becomes a direct line to the Echo network in Echo War — she can hear AEON-12's broadcasts and interpret them.
- Varyx's survival and disposition determines a major faction at the Echo War Diplomatic Summit — with him as an ally, the Wardens have 200+ enhanced soldiers; without him, they fight alone.
- The Frostfire Weapon Blueprint (if recovered) gives players advantage on checks to understand or counteract Aevorian draconic modifications in Echo War's Command Center.

## Escalations

- If players skip all negotiation and go straight to combat in Scene 4: The combat is survivable but hard; have Kaelith intervene at 50% of Varyx's HP — *"Enough. You've made your point. All of you."* She can broker a temporary ceasefire that reopens negotiation.
- If players kill Varyx: The Clan immediately fractures. Kaelith (if alive) takes temporary charge and can still be negotiated with, but the 200 soldiers are not an option for Echo War. Some warriors pursue the party as a recurring threat.
- If players get stuck in the tundra or lose track of where to go: The Echo-Weaver scouts (if not driven off) deliver a formal invitation from Varyx directly. He's been watching since the raid. He *wants* this meeting.
- If no one in the party has Arcana for the Weapon scene: Kaelith can provide the shutdown code — she's been waiting for an excuse to use it. Her loyalty is to the Clan's survival, not the Weapon's activation.

## Season 1 Callbacks

- **[Temple of the Dragonknights]** — The Clan's "birthright" claim: the Temple was the Clan's home for a millennium after the Great Fall; the Wardens' Season 1 investigation broke the seals the Clan had maintained. From Varyx's perspective, the party is the reason the Echo woke up. He's not wrong.
- **[Peril in Pinebrook]** — The raid returns to Pinebrook: Season 1 had the party defending this settlement. Now it's being attacked again, but by people with a legitimate grievance against the Aevorian artifacts in its basement. The players' Season 1 success left those artifacts there.
- **[Temple of the Dragonknights — draconic weapons]** — The "Wyrmscourge Missiles" and "Domination Protocols" that the party may have encountered or sealed in Season 1 are exactly what the Frostfire Weapon's Phase Two activates. Name them specifically if the party interacted with them.
- **[Marshal Brenna Thorne]** — Brenna's secret knowledge finally surfaces: she knew the Clan was in the north and chose not to act. This is a significant moment for her character — her competence has a shadow, and the party learns it here.
