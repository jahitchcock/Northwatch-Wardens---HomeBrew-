# DM Guide V3: Information Hierarchy & Content Placement Rules

*Internal Documentation for DM Guide Consistency*

---

## Overview

This document establishes **content placement rules**, **navigation principles**, and **information hierarchy** for the DM Guide V3 project. It serves as the authoritative source for where content belongs and how it connects throughout all 9 phases of development.

**Purpose:** Maintain consistency across 51 tasks and 9 phases so that:
1. Information doesn't duplicate unnecessarily
2. Content is in the place players/DMs expect to find it
3. Navigation flows logically from beginner to expert
4. Cross-references prevent circular pathways

**Document Authority:** This hierarchy was designed during Phase 1 and should guide all decisions through Phase 9. Major deviations require project lead discussion.

---

## Content Categories & Placement Rules

### 1. CAMPAIGN FOUNDATION (Chapter 1-3)
**Purpose:** Establish context and campaign structure  
**Audience:** All DMs (required reading)  
**Format:** Narrative + Reference hybrid  
**Tone:** Welcoming, overview-focused

**What Goes Here:**
- World introduction (Aevoria, Northreach setting)
- Campaign philosophy (why the modular design works)
- Session 0 guidance (how to begin)
- High-level structural overview (how adventures interconnect)
- Guild organization (leadership structure)

**What Does NOT Go Here:**
- Detailed NPC personalities (save for Chapter 4 roster)
- Full adventure text (save for Chapter 5)
- Secret lore (save for Chapter 6)
- Reference tables (save for Chapter 7)
- Tactical combat guidance (save for adventure modules)

**Chapter 1 Specifically:**
- What is Aevoria? (world basics)
- What is Northwatch Wardens? (campaign elevator pitch)
- What is Season 1? (scope of this campaign)
- How do I use this guide? (navigation overview)

**Chapter 2 Specifically:**
- First DM? Start here (assumptions about reader)
- Session 0 checklist (30-60 min prep)
- Create your first characters (guild member basics)
- Run your first scene (opening encounter)
- Answers to common first questions

**Chapter 3 Specifically:**
- Campaign framework (modular design, order-independence)
- All adventures introduced by name & theme
- Guild structure with leadership names & roles
- Geography & locations (canonical confirmed)
- Campaign timeline (how long does a full campaign take?)

### 2. DM PREP & REFERENCE TOOLS (Chapter 4)
**Purpose:** Provide tools for session preparation  
**Audience:** All DMs (reference while prepping)  
**Format:** Pure reference (tables, stat blocks, lists)  
**Tone:** Concise, actionable, jargon-clear

**What Goes Here:**
- Campaign arc definition (how adventures sequence)
- NPC rosters (stats, motivations, secrets referenced but not spoiled)
- Campaign mechanics (rank system, reputation, seasonal events)
- Session prep templates (checklists, trackers)
- Encounter tables (travel, downtime, random)
- Mystery tracking tools (clue database, revelation pacing)
- Tactical DM notes (when to use certain mechanics)
- NPC relationship diagrams (visual connection maps)

**What Does NOT Go Here:**
- Narrative adventure text (save for Chapter 5)
- DM-only spoiler lore (save for Chapter 6)
- Beginner guidance without tactical context (save for Chapter 2)
- World flavor text meant for read-aloud (save for adventure modules)
- Adventure-specific encounters (save for Chapter 5)

**Organization Within Chapter 4:**
- Campaign Planning (arc, mystery planning, foreshadowing database)
- NPC Resources (rosters, relationship diagrams, quick cards)
- Campaign Mechanics (rank system, seasonal events, faction response)
- Session Tools (prep checklist, campaign tracker, what-if guide)
- Encounter Library (travel, downtime, quick references)
- Adventure Overview (one-page summary per adventure linking to Chapter 5)

**Key Principle:** Chapter 4 is "tools for running your game," not "fluff or lore." If it doesn't help a DM prep or run a session, it belongs elsewhere.

---

### 3. ADVENTURE MODULES (Chapter 5)
**Purpose:** Complete, ready-to-run adventure text with all necessary information  
**Audience:** DMs running that specific adventure  
**Format:** Linear narrative (read front to back, then reference mid-session)  
**Tone:** Detailed, atmospheric, tactical

**What Goes Here (Per Adventure):**
- Overview (theme, level range, sessions estimate, mystery integration)
- Scene-by-scene text (read-aloud + tactical notes)
- NPC statistics (full stat blocks for creatures in that adventure)
- Encounter design (scaling notes for party size)
- Mystery clues (where they appear, how to track them)
- Consequences (what changes based on player choices)
- Prep checklist (specific to that adventure)
- Maps & tactical aids (battle maps, location layout)
- Links to Chapter 4 (where to find NPC details, mechanical support)
- Links to Chapter 6 (what DMs know secretly about that adventure)

**What Does NOT Go Here:**
- Campaign-wide mystery explanation (save for Chapter 3 Dashboard)
- Long NPC biographies (summarize in-adventure; detail in Chapter 4)
- Unrelated adventures (each module is self-contained)
- Repetitive campaign rules (reference Chapter 4 instead)
- Non-adventure content (side quests keep own structures)

**Organization Within Each Adventure Module:**
1. Quick Reference (1 page: level, sessions, clues, consequences)
2. Adventure Overview (narrative summary)
3. Scene 1-X (detailed scene structure)
4. NPC Statistics (stat blocks for that adventure)
5. Encounter Scaling (how to adjust for party size)
6. Treasure & Rewards (magic items, loot, experience)
7. Running This Adventure (common questions, pacing tips)
8. Consequence Tracker (what changes in the world)

### 4. DM SECRETS & LORE (Chapter 6)
**Purpose:** Complete campaign secrets and hidden lore (DM knowledge only)  
**Audience:** DMs only (SPOILER WARNING)  
**Format:** Reference + narrative hybrid  
**Tone:** Mysterious, detailed, lore-focused

**What Goes Here:**
- Complete mystery explanation (Aevorian Echo full definition)
- NPC secrets and hidden motivations
- Location lore (what's hidden in places)
- Faction agendas (what factions really want)
- Timeline of events (how things came to be)
- Consequence cascades (how each adventure changes the world)
- Alternative paths (secrets not revealed unless PCs find them)
- Long-term plot hooks (how campaign continues past Season 1)

**What Does NOT Go Here:**
- Any information players should discover (save spoilers for actual revelation)
- Beginner DM guidance (save for Chapter 2)
- Session prep tools (save for Chapter 4)
- Adventure-specific mechanics (save for Chapter 5)
- Campaign overview (save for Chapter 3)

**Spoiler Management:**
- Every section opens with "SPOILER: This section reveals X"
- Subsections marked [SPOILER] warn about specific reveals
- Links to which adventure reveals each secret (so DMs know timing)
- Guidance on "when to tell players" for each secret

### 5. QUICK REFERENCE & APPENDIX (Chapter 7)
**Purpose:** Lookup tables, glossaries, indices for rapid information retrieval  
**Audience:** All DMs (reference while running)  
**Format:** Pure reference (no narrative, pure structure)  
**Tone:** Neutral, dense, information-first

**What Goes Here:**
- Alphabetical index (all major topics with page numbers)
- Glossary (D&D terms, campaign-specific vocabulary)
- Quick-reference tables (equipment, pricing, NPC contact info)
- Maps (Northreach geography, settlement layouts)
- Character sheets (blank templates if needed)
- Visual diagrams (mystery connection chart, adventure flow)
- Common questions reference (FAQ organized by topic)
- Where to find X (topic index helping new DMs navigate)

**What Does NOT Go Here:**
- Narrative content (save for appropriate chapter)
- Detailed descriptions (save for relevant chapter)
- Campaign rules that need context (reference Chapter 4 instead)
- Adventure-specific information (save for Chapter 5)

## Navigation & Cross-Reference Rules

### Linking Between Chapters

**From Chapter 1-3 (Foundation):**
- Link to Chapter 4 for detailed tools
- Link to Chapter 5 for adventure text
- Link to Chapter 7 for quick lookups
- **Never** link to Chapter 6 (spoilers)

**From Chapter 4 (Prep Tools):**
- Link to Chapter 3 for campaign overview context
- Link to Chapter 5 for adventure specifics
- Link to Chapter 6 for NPC secrets (marked as spoilers)
- Link to Chapter 7 for quick reference tables

**From Chapter 5 (Adventures):**
- Link to Chapter 4 for NPC details / mechanics support
- Link to Chapter 6 for adventure-specific secrets (marked as spoilers)
- Link to other adventures only for short callouts ("see also: X adventure")
- Link to Chapter 7 for maps & quick reference

**From Chapter 6 (Secrets):**
- Link to Chapter 5 (which adventure reveals this)
- Link to Chapter 4 (which NPC knows this)
- Link to Chapter 3 (campaign arc context)
- **Never** link from Chapter 6 backward to earlier sections (one-way spoiler prevention)

**From Chapter 7 (Appendix):**
- Link to any chapter as reference lookup (it's the index)
- Brief entries only; detail references forward to appropriate chapter

### Avoiding Circular References

**Problem:** "For details on this NPC, see Chapter 4. For the adventure they appear in, see Chapter 5. For their secrets, see Chapter 6."
**Solution:** Chapter 4 entry says "appears in [linked adventure]"; Chapter 5 adventure says "for details see Chapter 4 [NPC name]"; Chapter 6 says "reveals the truth about NPC [linked to Chapter 4]"

**Test:** If a DM follows links in order, they should never be sent backward through a guide (except from Chapter 7 appendix).

## Content Priority Hierarchy

When content could fit in multiple places, use this priority:

1. **Where it's first discovered** (if players discover NPC info during adventure, include in Chapter 5, then link to Chapter 4 for full details)
2. **Where it's most useful** (if a tool is needed primarily during prep, Chapter 4; primarily during session, with the adventure)
3. **Where it naturally supports other content** (if information supports campaign arc, Chapter 3; supports an adventure, Chapter 5)
4. **Where it's referenced most** (if a table is used in 3 adventures, keep it in Chapter 4 as a reference)

## Beginner vs. Expert Content Placement

### Beginner Content (DMs new to D&D or Northwatch)
- **Location:** Early in each chapter, clearly labeled
- **Format:** Simpler language, more explanations, fewer assumptions
- **Examples:** Chapter 2 (Session 0 guide), Campaign Overview sections, Quick Reference tables
- **Tone:** Welcoming, reassuring, step-by-step

**Pattern:** Every chapter should have a "New DM?" section addressing common beginner questions immediately.

### Expert Content (DMs with campaign experience)
- **Location:** Later in relevant chapters, assumes familiarity
- **Format:** Dense tables, complex mechanics, lore depth
- **Examples:** NPC secrets (Chapter 4 roster section), Faction agendas, Consequence cascades
- **Tone:** Detailed, sophisticated, reference-focused

**Pattern:** Complex tools in Chapter 4 should include examples or quick-start guides for new DMs, then detailed mechanics for experts.

### Transitional Content (bridges beginner and expert)
- **Location:** Middle of each chapter
- **Format:** Simple explanation + complex reference
- **Examples:** Campaign arc overview with detailed history, NPC list with relationship diagram, Mystery clue banking with revelation guide
- **Tone:** Accessible but thorough

**Pattern:** Always show "here's how this works simply, here's how it works in depth."

## Content Duplication Rules

### Intentional Duplication
These items duplicate intentionally for usability:

1. **NPC Names:** Appear in Chapter 4 roster, Chapter 6 secrets, relevant adventures (Chapter 5), and appendix (Chapter 7)
   - **Why:** Fast lookup from any location

2. **Mystery Clues:** Listed in Chapter 4 mystery tracking tool AND appear in adventure text (Chapter 5) AND explained in Chapter 6 (secrets)
   - **Why:** DMs need to track clues during prep AND see them in-session AND understand full significance

3. **Maps:** May appear in Chapter 5 (adventure-specific) AND Chapter 7 (appendix quick reference)
   - **Why:** DMs need them when running that specific adventure AND want them accessible all at once

4. **Campaign Timeline:** In Chapter 3 (campaign overview) AND Chapter 6 (secrets with full dates/context)
   - **Why:** DMs need basic timeline for understanding AND detailed historical context for secrets

### Prohibited Duplication
These should appear in only ONE location:

1. **Campaign Rules** (Rank system, faction mechanics, seasonal calendar) → Chapter 4 only (link from adventures)
2. **Beginning DM Guidance** → Chapter 2 only (link from other chapters when relevant)
3. **Adventure Text** → Chapter 5 only (reference, don't repeat)
4. **NPC Full Biographies** → Chapter 4 only (summarize elsewhere, link to full text)
5. **Mystery Lore** → Chapter 6 only for full explanation (tease in other chapters, fully explain once)

## Tone & Voice Consistency

### Chapter-by-Chapter Tone Guide

| Chapter | Tone | Voice | Example |
|---------|------|-------|---------|
| 1 | Welcoming | Conversational, upbeat | "Welcome to Aevoria—let's build something memorable together." |
| 2 | Supportive | Encouraging, step-by-step | "You can do this. Here are the 5 things you need to know." |
| 3 | Professional | Clear, organized, authoritative | "The campaign operates on three core principles: modularity, consequence, and mystery." |
| 4 | Practical | Actionable, concise, reference-focused | "Marshal Brenna Thorne | Human Fighter | AC 16 | Motivation: Protect the frontier" |
| 5 | Atmospheric | Detailed, narrative, evocative | "As dawn breaks, frost clings to the creek stones in geometric patterns you can't quite explain." |
| 6 | Mysterious | Knowing, detailed, lore-rich | "The Aevorian Echo originates from Salsvault, a pre-Calamity research facility buried beneath the glacier." |
| 7 | Neutral | Data-focused, index-like | "Salsvault: Far north of Palebank Village. Aevorian ruin. Appears in: The Pale Sickness" |

**Rule:** Tone shifts should match content purpose, not be random. A table in Chapter 4 should never sound like narrative prose; adventure text in Chapter 5 should never sound like a reference table.

## Visual Hierarchy & Formatting

### Markdown Hierarchy (Consistent Throughout)

```markdown
# Chapter Title (H1 — Chapter level)
## Section (H2 — major topic)
### Subsection (H3 — specific guidance)
#### Detail (H4 — reference level)
```

**Never skip hierarchy levels** (don't jump from H1 to H3).

### Visual Markers

- **Bold:** Emphasis on critical concepts or names
- `Code:` Used for game mechanics, ability names, proper nouns when referencing in tables
- *Italic:* Used for read-aloud text or player-facing content
- > Blockquotes: Used for read-aloud text or important callouts
- Boxes/Sidebars: Used for tips, warnings, or special information

### Tables

- Use tables for comparative reference (NPC list, adventure selector, etc.)
- Include headers for every table
- Keep tables to 5+ rows (fewer rows should be bullet list)

### Lists

- Use unordered lists for options (no inherent order)
- Use ordered lists for sequential steps or priority ranking
- Keep list items to 1-2 lines (anything longer should be a paragraph)

## Content Development Process (For Phase 2-9 Work)

When creating new content:

1. **Identify the problem** — What question does this content answer?
2. **Determine the chapter** — Using hierarchy rules above, where does it belong?
3. **Check for duplication** — Is this information already elsewhere? Should there be links instead?
4. **Define the audience** — Beginner, Expert, or both?
5. **Choose the format** — Narrative, table, quick reference, or hybrid?
6. **Select the tone** — Match chapter tone from guide above
7. **Plan cross-references** — Where should DMs be sent for related info?
8. **Create the content** — Write/design the material
9. **Add navigation** — Include chapter links, back-to-top, related info pointers
10. **Review hierarchy** — Does this fit logically in its chapter? Can it be found easily?

## Special Content Types & Their Placement

### NPC Quick Cards
**Location:** Chapter 4 (as searchable list)  
**Format:** 1-page summary per NPC (AC, HP, personality, motivation, secrets flagged)  
**Cross-Reference:** Link to Chapter 5 (which adventures they appear in), Chapter 6 (their secrets)

### Stat Blocks
**Location:** Chapter 5 (adventures they appear in) + Chapter 4 (NPC roster)  
**Format:** Game Master 5e XML or Homebrewery markdown  
**Note:** Same stat block appears in both places; if updated, update in both locations

### Clue Database
**Location:** Chapter 4 (Mystery Revelation Pacing section)  
**Format:** Table with columns: Clue | Where Found | What It Reveals | When to Plant | Related Clues  
**Cross-Reference:** Link to Chapter 5 (which adventure contains it), Chapter 6 (full explanation)

### Maps & Diagrams
**Location by Type:**
- Adventure-specific maps: Chapter 5  
- Campaign-wide maps: Chapter 7 (Appendix)  
- Relationship diagrams: Chapter 4  
- Mystery connection charts: Chapter 7 (Appendix)

### Consequence Trackers
**Location:** Chapter 5 (per adventure) AND Chapter 4 (consolidated)  
**Format:** Table showing "If players choose X, then Y changes in the world"  
**Note:** Individual adventure shows its consequences; Chapter 4 shows how they interact

### Mystery Elements (Clues, Lore, Revelations)
**Location:** 
- **First mention:** Chapter 3 (Campaign Overview) as mystery teaser  
- **Clue location:** Chapter 5 (adventure text)  
- **Full explanation:** Chapter 6 (secrets)  
- **Tracking:** Chapter 4 (revelation pacing guide)

## Examples of Information Hierarchy in Action

### Example 1: An NPC (Portal Keeper Mera)

**Chapter 1-3:** Mentioned as "one of the leadership" in overview  
**Chapter 4 (NPC Roster):**
- 1-paragraph personality
- AC/HP/abilities summary
- Motivation: "Protect the frontier"
- Link: "Appears in: Wolves of Welton, Peril in Pinebrook"
- Link: "Secrets: See Chapter 6"  

**Chapter 5 (Wolves of Welton Adventure):**
- Full character description in first NPC encounter
- Dialogue examples
- Stat block if combat
- Consequence: "If Mera is convinced of wolf intelligence, she votes yes in council"
- Link: "For full NPC details, see Chapter 4 Roster"

**Chapter 6 (Secrets):**
- Mera's true motivation: She's researching the Echo for personal reasons
- Her connection to Salsvault
- What she suspects vs. what players discover
- Link: "First revealed in: Wolves of Welton"
- Link: "Related: See Marshal Thorne secrets"

**Chapter 7 (Appendix):**
- Index entry: "Mera, Portal Keeper | Appears in Ch5:Ch5(2), Ch6:p.XX"
- Quick reference: "Mera (human fighter) | In Welton | Wants information"

### Example 2: A Clue (Geometric Frost Pattern)

**Chapter 3 (Campaign Dashboard):**
- "Evidence of the Echo appears in many forms"

**Chapter 4 (Mystery Tracking Tool):**
- Clue: Geometric frost doesn't melt  
- Where: Creek approach to Welton (Wolves adventure) + Palebank (The Pale Sickness adventure)  
- What It Reveals: "Something unnatural is present"  
- DM Truth: "This is the first sign of Aevorian manifestation"  
- Related Clues: Humming metal, awake animals

**Chapter 5 (Wolves of Welton):**
- Scene description: "Thin frost clings to creek stones in geometric patterns that don't melt despite the sun"
- DM note: "Let players investigate; this is a mystery clue"

**Chapter 5 (The Pale Sickness):**
- More obvious manifestation: "The entire glacier has these patterns, miles across"
- DM note: "Now players realize this isn't isolated"

**Chapter 6 (Secrets - The Aevorian Echo):**
- Full explanation: "These patterns are mathematical constructs generated by the Echo's spreading influence—it structures matter according to pre-Calamity algorithms"

**Chapter 7 (Appendix):**
- Mystery Chart: "Geometric Frost → First hint → Visible in multiple locations → Explained in The Pale Sickness"
