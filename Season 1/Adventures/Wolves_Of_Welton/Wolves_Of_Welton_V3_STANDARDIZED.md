## CHAPTER 1: ADVENTURE OVERVIEW

### Campaign Context

**Where This Fits**

### Adventure Premise

*A pastoral village awakens to an impossible threat: wolves displaying supernatural intelligence. Are these creatures victims, monsters, or something in between? Your choices will determine not only the fate of Welton, but also your first clue about something far larger stirring across the frontier.*

The wizened shepherd Corel whispers that the wolves *planned* their attacks. Father Merriksonn reveals his sorcerer brother vanished the night the attacks began. Village council members argue whether to hire adventurers or let fear drive their people away. And in the western woods, two awakened wolves debate their own survival, unaware that negotiation—or slaughter—awaits.

This is your campaign's opening mystery: a moral dilemma that rewards investigation, offers multiple solutions, and plants the first clue that something ancient has awakened in the Northreach frontier.

### Core Themes

1. **Moral Complexity:** Not all threats are evil; intelligence doesn't guarantee malice. Killing the wolves is easy—but is it right?
2. **Investigation Rewards:** Discovering the wolves' intelligence changes everything. Combat becomes negotiation; threat becomes potential alliance.
3. **Community Consequence:** Player choices reshape village politics. A successful negotiation makes the wolves allies; extermination closes off their future aid.
4. **Mystery Integration:** The wolves' awakening is a symptom of something larger. The clues point to Salsvault and the Aeorian Echo (revealed in Frozen Sick).
5. **Agency & Choice:** There is no "correct" ending. Extermination, alliance, compromise, or deception—each path has consequences players will carry forward.

### The Aeorian Echo Connection

*What Players Discover Here:*

Something magical caused wolves to become sapient overnight. They can speak, plan, and reason—impossible for animals. The awakening coincided with the disappearance of Alexi Merriksonn, a local sorcerer. The wolves possess strange magical residue (if detected).

If players investigate thoroughly, they may find ancient Aeorian symbols buried north of Welton.

*What Players Don't Know Yet:*

Alexi Merriksonn triggered a dormant Aeorian facility (Salsvault) while researching magical phenomena. The activation released a surge of transformative magic that killed him and, as a side effect, awakened the wolves' latent consciousness. This is not an isolated incident—other magical disturbances are occurring across Northreach (discovered in Frozen Sick, Wild Sheep Chase, Temple of DK).

The Echo is spreading. The wolves are the first visible symptom.

### What Players Know (Before Starting)

**If This Is the Campaign Opener:**
Newly created characters know only: they chartered with the Northwatch Wardens yesterday. A contract posted at Waystone Inn promised 800 gp for eliminating wolves plaguing Welton.

### Pacing Overview

**Session Breakdown** _(for 2–3 session structure)_

| Session | Content | Duration | Key Moment |
|---------|---------|----------|-----------|
| Session 1 | Scenes 1–2: Road encounter + Village arrival + Information gathering | 2–3 hours | First wolf combat; realize wolves aren't simple beasts |
| Session 2 | Scene 3–4: Investigation + Wolf tracking + Journey to den | 2–3 hours | Encounter with Owlbear (optional, adds tension); wolf den discovery |
| Session 3 | Scene 5–6: Wolf negotiation or final combat + Resolution | 2–3 hours | Council vote or combat resolution; world consequences established |

**Pacing Notes:**
- **Where players usually slow down:** Information gathering scene (many NPCs, lots of rumor-checking)
  - *Solution:* Provide 2–3 key information pieces quickly; have Corel offer to guide them rather than letting them plan endlessly

- **Where pacing usually accelerates:** Wolf negotiation (tension high, stakes clear)
  - *Solution:* Take a breath before council vote; let drama land

- **Where DMs get lost:** Scene 3 (wolf tracking is open-ended)
  - *Solution:* If players flounder, have Corel suggest "let's set a trap with your own bait" to move action forward

### Running as Single-Session One-Shot

**If condensing to ~4 hours for one-shot convention play:**

- **Skip:** Owlbear encounter (Scene 4) and detailed investigation legwork
- **Combine:** Scenes 1 & 2 (road encounter, then immediate village arrival)
- **Streamline:** Scene 3 by having Corel lead directly to wolf den (skip open-ended tracking)
- **Accelerate:** Scene 5 by introducing Bolt/Flame quickly in negotiation format
- **Reduce:** Council scene (if combat path) or streamline voting (if negotiation path)

**Estimated one-shot timing:** 3–4 hours, tighter pacing, fewer NPCs to track

### Common Pacing Issues & Solutions

| Problem | Why It Happens | Fix |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| **Investigation bogs down** | Too many NPCs, players talk to everyone | Provide +1 obvious clue quickly (Featherock says wolves were "talking"); offer Corel as immediate guide |
| **Players can't decide on approach** | Too many options, analysis paralysis | DM: "You're deliberating; what do you actually do first?" Nudge toward action |
| **Wolf combat feels lethal** | Low-level party, high wolf damage | Scale down wolf numbers (3–5 instead of 8) or use Bolt to yield early (at 50% HP) |
| **Council vote takes forever** | Uncertain how voting mechanics work | Simplify: "You've convinced Father Merriksonn and Corel (YES votes). Need 1 more. Tillus is the swing—pitch to him now." |
| **Owlbear encounter feels random** | Players unprepared, combat drag | Make Owlbear optional; can skip entirely if pacing tight. Foreshadow: "You hear heavy crashing in undergrowth" (gives prep warning) |

## SCENE 1: THE HUNTED ROAD

**Scene Purpose**
Introduce the wolf threat through action. Establish that these are no ordinary wolves—they work together with tactical precision. Deliver the campaign's first combat encounter.

**Time & Location**
- **Timing:** Campaign opening, midday
- **Location:** Road to Welton, ~30 miles west of Waystone Inn
- **Duration:** 15–25 minutes (initiate → combat → aftermath)
- **Atmosphere:** Mundane traveling disrupted by sudden violence; realize the threat is *organized*

### Read-Aloud Text

> *You've been traveling for two days through rolling grasslands dotted with farmsteads. The road dips and rises with the hills, and ahead you see a slight bend where it passes between an outcrop of gray rock and the border of a thick woodland. Beyond, you can see the white-painted buildings of Welton settling in a distant valley.*
>
> *The afternoon sun is warm, and your feet are tired. Then— a sharp *crack* of twigs from the woods ahead.*
>
> *Two men are driving a flock of sheep through the bend, accompanied by four sheepdogs. The men have suddenly frozen, clubs pulled from their belts. Their dogs bark frantically, backing away.*
>
> *Then the wolves come.*
>
> *Lean gray shapes explode from both sides of the road in a coordinated rush. There's no hesitation, no chaos—just pure predatory efficiency. The sheep scatter, bleating. The men shout. And the wolves—there are eight of them—pick their targets with unnatural precision.*

### Key Information

- **Location:** 150 feet away; open road with rock outcrop (provides half cover if players use it)
- **NPCs Present:** Two unnamed shepherds with sheepdogs (Commoner stat block, weak combatants)
- **Environmental Features:** Road, grassland (normal movement), rock outcrop, woods edge
- **Threat Level:** Dangerous but not lethal if party acts

### What Players Can Observe (No checks required)

- Eight wolves attacking in coordinated formation (not random mauling)
- Wolves focus on sheep first, not immediate killing of shepherds
- Pack tactics: wolves support each other, flank targets
- **DC 10 Wisdom (Insight):** Wolf attacks show *planning*, not blind hunger
- **DC 12 Wisdom (Nature):** "This level of coordination is unusual for normal wolves"
- If any wolf is slain and examined: scars, strange markings, auras of magic (if detect magic cast)

### Combat: Road Encounter

**Start of Combat**
- **Party Positioning:** 150 feet away (can charge closer or hold position)
- **Enemy Positioning:** Wolves scattered among sheep, attacking both livestock and shepherds
- **Surprise?** No; action is visible from distance

**Wolves Present**
- 8 **Wolves** (MM p341) attacking
- **Stat Block:** AC 13 | HP 11 (2d8+2) | Speed 40 ft | STR 12 (+1) DEX 15 (+2) CON 13 (+1) INT 3 (-4) WIS 12 (+1) CHA 6 (-2)
- **Skills:** Perception +3, Stealth +4
- **Challenge:** 1/4 (50 XP each)

**Tactics**
- **Wolves use Pack Tactics:** Advantage when flanking
- **Hit and Retreat:** Attack, then Disengage if bloodied (below 5 HP)
- **Guard Each Other:** Will not let you kill their packmates without response; wolves circle bloodied allies
- **Sheep Distraction:** Wolves prioritize easy kills (sheep) unless threatened directly

**Combat Scaling by Party Size**

| Party Size | Wolf Count | Adjustment | Notes |
|------------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| 2 players | 4 wolves | Reduce by half; one guards a shepherd | Keep it quick |
| 3 players | 6 wolves | Reduce by 1/4; most threat but manageable | Default scaling |
| 4–5 players | 8 wolves | Full encounter as written | Standard load; wolves may flee at halfway point |
| 5+ / Levels 2–3 | 10 wolves | Add 2 extra; introduce 1-2 **Dire Wolf** (MM) | Increase danger |

**Combat Flow**

*Round 1:* Wolves attack shepherds and sheep. Party rolls initiative.

*Rounds 2–3:* Wolves focus fire on party members (especially vulnerable ones). Shepherds retreat or try to help.

*Round 4+:* Once 3+ wolves are slain OR any wolf is severely wounded, remaining wolves reassess. **Roll Wisdom save (DC 10) for pack morale:** Success = wolves continue fighting; Failure = wolves disengage and retreat toward woods (allowing party victory without total annihilation).

**Combat Ends When:**
- All wolves slain (party victory, complete)
- Wolves flee (party victory, incomplete—threat remains)
- 4+ wolves killed (remaining wolves disengage)

### Aftermath: Shepherd Interaction

**If party helps (likely):**

Grateful shepherds introduce themselves as hired hands from Welton. They offer to guide party to village and provide lodging at Shepherd's Crook (inn). They share local gossip freely (see **Shepherd Rumors** in Scene 2).

**If party ignores (unlikely):**

Combat proceeds without NPC interference, but shepherds are still fleeing and can direct party to Welton afterward.

**Shepherd Names:** Davol and Wren (remember these for potential future references)

### Shepherd Rumors (if party chats)

> *"Course, all started when that sorcerer, Father Merriksonn's brother Alexi, went missing. Not that I'd hear a word against him, but it makes you think..."*

> *"Wolves appearing outside locked barns. Stealing entire herds in one night. Moving like they had... like they were planning it."*

> *"Village sent hunters after them. Some didn't come back. Those that did said something about wolves in the darkness arguing in... voices? Sounded mad, unless you believe it."*

> *"A shepherd named Featherock survived. Dumped on Westly's doorstep half-dead. Keeps babbling about hearing wolves talk. They think he's cracked."*

### Decision Point

**Party realizes:** Wolves aren't random threats—they're *organized.* Someone or something made them smarter. This isn't a typical hunt. It's a mystery demanding investigation.

### Common Questions for This Scene

**Q: "Can we just avoid the wolves and keep traveling?"**
A: Yes, you can. Roll a Dexterity saving throw (DC 12) to slip past using the rock outcrop as cover. You avoid combat but reach Welton with no information. (Suggest against this; info-gathering matters.)

**Q: "What if we try to communicate with the wolves?"**
A: The wolves are feral pack wolves, not awakened yet. They respond to aggressive humans with aggression. If you use *speak with animals* or similar magic, you hear base predatory instinct ("food," "threat," "pack"). No negotiation possible. (They're not intelligent until you meet Bolt.)

**Q: "Can we capture a wolf instead of killing it?"**
A: Difficult but possible. Requires grapple contest and successful restraint. You could bring a captured wolf to interrogation later (if magical communication possible). Good lateral thinking.

**Q: "How long does this combat take?"**
A: 4–6 minutes at table; 1–2 rounds in-game if party handles it efficiently.

### DM Notes

- **Tone:** This combat should feel dangerous but beatable. Don't let it drag; wolves are cowards and will flee if outmatched
- **Checkpoint:** If party is struggling (2+ members bloodied), let a wolf *accidentally* show intelligence (hesitates before attack, or whispers a warning to packmate in rough Common)
- **Discovery Hook:** This encounter plants a question: "These wolves fight like they can *think*"

## SCENE 2: WELTON & THE COUNCIL

**Scene Purpose**
Gather information and meet key NPCs. Party learns about wolf attacks, Alexi's disappearance, and the village's dire situation. Council asks party to help—and party gets to decide how.

**Time & Location**
- **Timing:** Late afternoon (after road encounter)
- **Location:** Welton village; Shepherd's Crook inn
- **Duration:** 60–90 minutes (travel + arrival + council scene)
- **Atmosphere:** Relief at reaching safety; tension of village under siege; hope that adventurers can help

### Read-Aloud Text: Arrival at Welton

> *As you descend into the valley, Welton reveals itself fully. Stone and timber buildings painted white, with carefully tended fields beyond. It's peaceful—but something is *wrong*. More people idling in streets than should be working. Faces marked with anxiety. Heavy wheel-ruts in mud, as if many have fled the outlying farms.*
>
> *At the village center stands the **Shepherd's Crook**—a large ancient inn with a prominent common room. Smoke rises from multiple chimneys. Inside, the room is crowded with shepherds in wool cloaks, speaking in low voices. A halfling innkeeper behind the bar looks up as you enter.*
>
> *"Adventurers! Praise the gods. We've been expecting you. The council is meeting in back—they need to speak with you immediately."*

### The Shepherd's Crook Inn

**NPC: Leanor Slatebeard** (female dwarf, 100+ years old, innkeeper)
- **Personality:** Warm, cheerful, hardworking; everyone's confidant
- **What She Offers:** Free meals and drinks for the party; gossip about every NPC in the village
- **Key Info:** "Best place to sleep and eat in Welton. We take good care of the Wardens here."

**Atmosphere:** Crowded, anxious, but welcoming. Smell of stew, woodsmoke, wet wool. Shepherds huddle in groups; occasional raised voice, quickly quieted.

**Available to Chat:**
- Davol and Wren (if they guided you here) will introduce you to other shepherds
- Leanor offers to direct you to any NPC
- Local gossip available freely (check Scene 1 Rumor list—party may hear these again)

### The Council Meeting Room

**Time:** Late afternoon; council has been in session for hours

**NPCs Present**
- **Father Merriksonn** — village priest, human male, mid-50s
- **Tillus Merrion** — halfling council leader, businessman, blustering
- **Corel** — human shepherd, weathered, quiet, respected
- **4 other council members** — representatives of various guilds (less important, but present)

**Current Conflict (when party arrives):**

Father Merriksonn and Tillus are *arguing bitterly* over whether village should halt exports to preserve food. Merriksonn says "We'll starve if we can afford to export"; Tillus says "We'll go bankrupt if we don't."

Party interruption is *welcome relief*.

### What the Council Wants

**Immediately:**

"Is Contract W-17 fulfilled? Are you the adventurers sent by Waystone Inn?" (simple verification)

**Core Request:**

"Kill the wolves plaguing Welton. We'll pay 800 gold pieces and provide lodging + supplies."

**Full Explanation:**

- Wolves have been attacking for 3+ weeks
- Started with small livestock raids; escalated to organized herd theft
- Village sent a hunting party; were ambushed and drove back (some members injured or missing)
- At least one person (Featherock) survived attack and is still recovering at inn
- Wolves display unusual intelligence (open locked gates, coordinate attacks, even *seemed to communicate*)
- Local sorcerer (Alexi Merriksonn, Father Merriksonn's brother) vanished at start of attacks—coincidence?
- Village is losing farmers to fear; bankrupting themselves meeting export contracts

**What They Know:**
- Wolves lair somewhere to the west in the woods
- Corel can guide anyone tracking them (he does)
- No one has actually seen the full pack—estimates 6–10 wolves
- "Some of the men swear the wolves were *talking* to each other. Mad, probably."

### Scene 2 Information Gathering

**Party Can Ask About:**

**The Wolves:**
- "Attack patterns suggest *planning*" (Corel)
- "Open gates from inside barns—seems impossible" (Tillus)
- "Maybe they're possessed? Cursed?" (general speculation, no one knows)
- **DC 12 Arcana check:** Someone suggests "magical tampering" uneasily

**Alexi Merriksonn (the missing sorcerer):**
- Disappeared 2–3 weeks ago (timeline matches wolf attacks)
- Was Father Merriksonn's brother
- Studied "transformation magic" and was researching magical phenomena
- Went north (toward wild areas) to investigate something; never returned
- **Father Merriksonn's Fear:** "His magic caused this. I don't know how, but I feel it."
- **Available for deeper chat:** Father will speak privately with party about Alexi later (in room above stairs)

**Featherock (traumatized survivor):**
- Halfling shepherd, badly injured in hunting party ambush
- Recovered in room above inn
- Keeps raving about hearing wolves *argue* and discuss "whether to eat him"
- Most dismiss him as shock-damaged; Father Merriksonn listens seriously
- Party can visit him (Leanor points to his room): See **Scene 2B: Featherock's Room** below

**The Village's Desperation:**
- Several farming families already fled to city
- Livestock losses are catastrophic
- Council needs solution *soon* or village will economically collapse
- "Kill them or we all starve" (various council members, repeatedly)

### Scene 2B: Optional — Featherock's Room

**If party wants to chat with injured survivor:**

> *A halfling lies in bed, pale and trembling. Bandages wrap his left arm (bitten), his right leg (trampled). Fresh flowers sit on a dresser. He looks up at you with hollow, haunted eyes.*

**NPC: Willen Featherock** (halfling shepherd, traumatized)
- **Initial State:** Defensive, afraid; "Who are you? Is it safe?"
- **After Rapport:** Will share everything he remembers (hazy from shock)
- **Key Info:**
  - Hunting party was ambushed in the woods (wolves appeared everywhere at once)
  - Featherock was bitten badly, left behind as hunters fled
  - Consciousness was fading, but he heard *voices*. Wolves. Arguing.
  - "Should we eat him or release him?" "He has value alive." "What value has prey?"
  - **Most Important:** "They were *thinking*. Debating. Using *words*."

**If Party Believes Him:**
- Featherock cries with relief ("Finally, someone believes me!")
- Later, if negotiation succeeds, Featherock recovers fast and becomes grateful ally
- He'll warn others: "The wolves are intelligent. They're not mindless beasts."

**If Party Dismisses Him:**
- Featherock retreats into himself
- Later, if you meet intelligent wolves, no bonus from his credibility

### Council Voting & Consequences (Preview)

**Important for later:** Party will present solutions to this council. Council members have positions:

- **Father Merriksonn:** Will vote YES for negotiation (if given chance); believes wolves' intelligence means they deserve opportunity
- **Corel:** Will vote YES for most reasonable solutions; he's thoughtful and open
- **Tillus Merrion:** Leans NO; wants problems solved permanently (killed); can be swayed with economic arguments
- **4 Other Council Members:** Neutral; persuadable with good arguments; each has 1 vote

**Preview for DM:** If party kills all wolves, council pays immediately (800 gp). If negotiation attempted, council vote determines outcome (need 3+ YES votes for approval).

### Rewards Available (Scene 2 only)

**800 gold pieces** (from council, paid whenever task "complete")
- Up front if wolves killed
- After probationary period if alliance forged
- Withheld if party doesn't actually deal with threat

**Free lodging** at Shepherd's Crook indefinitely (standard inn room)

**Supplies:** Leanor offers to sell healing potions (50 gp each), food rations (5 gp each)

### Consequences & Next Step

**Party Leaves with:**
- Clear mission (deal with wolves)
- Multiple possible approaches (kill, negotiate, trap, investigate)
- Key NPCs identified (Father Merriksonn, Corel offer help)
- Mystery hook (Alexi's disappearance + wolf intelligenc)
- Optional guide (Corel volunteers; can be hired or join willingly)

**If Party Tries to Leave Without Knowing Anything:**
- Leanor prevents: "Wait! You at least need to know where to look!"
- Gives basic info: "Wolves lair in woods to the west. Corel can guide you."

### Common Questions for This Scene

**Q: "Can we just stake out the farm and ambush returning wolves?"**
A: Good tactical idea! Corel offers his flock as bait (Scene 3 option). Wolves take it—this works.

**Q: "How much do we actually know about the wolves at this point?"**
A: Only rumors and speculation. Unusual intelligence, but no confirmation they can *negotiate*. Party will discover this through investigation or direct encounter.

**Q: "Can we talk to Alexi to understand what happened?"**
A: Alexi is missing (possibly dead). Father Merriksonn will share what he knows (his brother studied transformation magic, went to research something, never returned). Party doesn't learn truth until Frozen Sick adventure reveals Salsvault.

### DM Notes

- **Tone:** Village is desperate but civil. No hostility toward party; only hope
- **Pacing:** If council meeting/information gathering drags, jump to "Well, you've got what you need—what's your plan?" to move toward investigation/hunting
- **Character Moments:** Allow private conversations (Featherock's trauma, Father Merriksonn's grief, Corel's quiet wisdom). These NPCs matter for future adventures
- **Mystery Hook:** Alexi's disappearance and wolves' intelligence should feel *connected*. Plant seed: "Something magical happened at the same time Alexi vanished. Coincidence?"

## SCENE 3: SEEKING THE PACK

**Scene Purpose**
Party investigates, gathers clues, and travels to wolf den. This scene is flexible—multiple approaches work (tracking, trapping, following rumors).

**Time & Location**
- **Timing:** Next day (party rests overnight at inn)
- **Location:** Welton area; western woods
- **Duration:** Variable; 2–4 hours (investigation) + 4 hours (travel through woods) = 6–8 hours total
- **Atmosphere:** Countryside investigation; growing sense of stalking unseen threat

### Party Approaches to Wolf Finding

**Option A: Direct Tracking**
Party follows wolf tracks/scent. Requires **Survival check (DC 14)** to follow fresh tracks, **DC 16** if tracks are 8+ hours old.

Corel can join and provide +4 bonus to Survival checks if party asks (he knows the woods). With Corel's help, DC drops to 10–13.

**Time:** 4–6 hours to reach den

**Option B: Trap & Wait**
Use Corel's flock as bait. Set ambush at predictable location. Wolves come to feeding ground (guaranteed in 1–2 hours). Party observes from hiding, then follows or engages.

**Time:** 1–2 hours

**Option C: Information From Locals (Westly's Farm)**
Visit Westly's Farm (where initial attacks occurred, near Old Woods). Westly remembers seeing wolf tracks leading north into old ruins. Can point party in right direction.

**Time:** 2–3 hours (if taking detour)

**Option D: Rest & Let Wolves Come**
If party camps at edge of woods, wolves may approach for evening hunt. Corel warns: "They'll find you if you're out here."

**Time:** Unpredictable; possible random encounter

### Skill Checks & Clue Discovery

**Tracking Wolves Through Woods**
- **Survival DC 14:** Fresh tracks point toward western lair (leads to den)
- **Nature DC 12:** Wolf behavior unusual—organized, not random hunting paths
- **Investigation DC 13:** Find remains of killed livestock; signs of pack cooperation

**Searching Featherock's Ambush Site** (if party investigates scene of attack)
- **Investigation DC 12:** Find wolf fur with scorch marks and claw marks (suggests magical combat; confirms Alexi was here)
- **Arcana DC 14:** "Transmutation magic was cast here—recently. This was a magical fight."

**If Visiting Westly's Farm**
- **Perception DC 13:** Buried pieces of metal or strange symbols in ground nearby
- **Arcana DC 15:** "These symbols match pre-Calamity designs. Aeorian. This land was inhabited by ancient people."
- **History/Arcana DC 16:** "This is an archaeological site. These ruins predate the current kingdom by centuries."

**All Clues Point Here:** Magic was involved. Alexi was involved. Something ancient is stirring.

### Optional Combat: The Owlbear (Scene 4)

**Trigger:** If party is traveling through Dense Woods at certain point, they encounter a wounded Owlbear.

**Read-Aloud Text:**

> *After two hours in the thick woods, you hear snapping branches and heavy crashing. Something *big* is stumbling through the undergrowth toward you.*
>
> *An enormous creature emerges—part owl, part bear, wholly terrifying. It's thin, limping, nursing deep wounds. Its fur is matted with old blood. Worse, you see deep *claw marks*—fresh ones. Whatever did this... it was no ordinary predator.*

**NPC: Wounded Owlbear** (MM p249, modified)
- **AC:** 14 (natural armor)
- **HP:** 40 (reduced from normal 59 due to injuries)
- **Threat Level:** Dangerous for party; lethal if unprepared
- **Motivation:** Starving, desperate, in pain—will attack party if provoked or threatened

**Combat Tactics:**
- Owlbear charges nearest target
- Fights until defeated or party flees
- Will NOT retreat; fights to death

**After Combat (if Owlbear Slain):**
- **Nature DC 12:** Claw marks are from *wolves*—unusual prey for an Owlbear
- **Perception DC 10:** Scorch marks on fur (magical fire damage)
- **Nature/Arcana DC 14:** "Wolves attacked an *Owlbear*. That's rare—Owlbears are nocturnal predators. These should be active at night, not day."

**Clue Significance:** Wolves are organized *and powerful* enough to challenge apex predators. This isn't a normal wolf pack.

**Optional:** Party can skip Owlbear encounter entirely (make it a random encounter option, not mandatory). If pacing is tight, DM can say "You navigate around the beast area and keep moving."

### Travel Through Woods: Descriptive Details

> *The western woods are old and thick. Mossy ground muffles your footsteps. Trees tower overhead, their branches creating a canopy that filters the daylight to a green-tinged haze. The smell of earth and decay fills the air. Every few minutes, a howl echoes from somewhere to the west—distant, but always present.*
>
> *Corel moves through the undergrowth with practiced ease, pointing out fresh tracks: paw prints, bent branches, signs of passage. The wolves have been here. Recently. Often."*

**Crossing Woods Time:** 4 hours at walking pace (can be reduced to 2–3 hours if party pushes hard (Constitution save DC 12) and risks making noise; loud travel may trigger random encounter).

### Arrival at Wolf Den

**Transition:**

> *After nearly six hours of travel, the forest begins to thin. Ahead, you see a clearing on the slope of a low hill. At the base of the hill is a *cave entrance*—10 feet wide, 10 feet tall—set into gray stone. Smoke plumes from somewhere on the hill above (the back exit).*
>
> *As you carefully approach the treeline, Corel puts a hand up to halt you.*
>
> *"There. The wolves' home."*
>
> *And indeed, milling around the cave entrance, you see wolves. Six of them. Some are lounging near a fire. Some appear to be *working*—repairing a crude fence. Some are tearing apart a sheep carcass.*
>
> *But what strikes you most is the colors. The wolves are wearing decorations—strips of bright cloth tied around limbs and necks. And around the cave, the ground is marked with crude drawings in ash and charcoal.*
>
> *Once, you would have called this a beast's lair. Now? It looks almost like a home.*

### Wolf Den: First Observations

**Without entering combat immediately, party can observe:**
- 6 wolves visible (not all pack members are outside)
- Wolves are wearing decorations (suggests: intelligence, organization, aesthetics)
- Drawings on ground depict hunting scenes—including one that shows humans
- Tripwire alarm system set at treeline (**Perception DC 13** to spot)
- Small fire with smoke plume (back exit leads to open hill)

**If party triggers tripwire:**
- Bone noisemakers clatter
- Wolves' heads snap up
- Howls echo from inside cave
- **Flame** and **Bolt** emerge within 1 round
- Combat likely

**If party approaches carefully (Stealth check DC 13):**
- Can observe wolves longer
- May overhear Flame and Bolt *arguing* inside cave about next actions (if party listens at back hole entrance)
- Better chance at negotiation if party demonstrates non-hostility

### Transition to Scene 5

Party has found the den and made first contact (visual). Next scene (Scene 5) covers the actual wolf *interaction*—whether that's combat, negotiation, or hybrid approach.

### Common Questions for This Scene

**Q: "How long does tracking take realistically?"**
A: Depends on approach. Corel + good Survival roll = 4–6 hours. Random searching = 8+ hours (DM can zoom past with "you spend a day and find it"). Random encounter + combat = adds 1–2 hours.

**Q: "Can we sneak all the way to the cave without being seen?"**
A: Difficult but possible. **Stealth check DC 13 vs. wolves' Perception DC 13.** If you win, you observe wolves for 1 minute before they sense you.

**Q: "What if we try an indirect approach—setting fire to the woods to drive them out?"**
A: Creative but destructive. Possible—but region is dry; fire could spread. Corel warns: "That will bring trouble beyond wolves." Consequences later (angered locals, possibly state investigation).

### DM Notes

- **Pacing:** Scene 3 is flexible. If players are decisive, rush through to get to wolf den. If they dawdle, drag it out with random encounters and atmospheric details
- **Tone:** Growing anticipation. The wolves are no longer distant—they're *near*. Danger feels real
- **Discovery:** By end of scene, party should realize: "These aren't just organized beasts. They've built a civilization." This realization shifts approaching the den from "mission to kill threats" to "about to meet a people"

## SCENE 5: THE DEN & THE PARLEY

**Scene Purpose**
Party makes contact with Bolt and Flame. Depending on party choices, this is either combat or negotiation moment—or both. This is the heart of the adventure: a moral choice with consequences.

**Time & Location**
- **Timing:** Arrival time (late afternoon or evening)
- **Location:** Wolf Den cave complex
- **Duration:** 30–60 minutes (before combat OR negotiation concludes)
- **Atmosphere:** Tense standoff; revelation that wolves are *sapient*; moral weight

### The Den Layout

**Main Cave Complex:**
- Central chamber: Living space, crude beds, charcoal drawings on walls
- Eastern sub-chamber: Meeting room, small fire, escape route (hole to outdoors)
- Western sub-chamber: Storeroom, stolen goods, caged sheep
- Northern sub-chamber: Nursery, wolf pups, 2 guardian wolves

**Key Features:**
- **Back Entrance:** Hole in cave ceiling 30 feet back, 15 feet up—just big enough for Medium creature to squeeze through
- **Front Entrance:** 10 ft wide, 10 ft tall cave mouth opening onto grassland
- **Interior:** Torch-lit (wolves can see in dark; they have torches for humanoid visitors and to better see during planning)

### Initial Contact: Your Approach Determines Their Reaction

**If Party Triggers Tripwire Alarm OR Approaches Openly:**

> *Before you can take another step, the wolves' heads snap up. A *howl* echoes from the darkness of the cave. The six wolves visible leap to their feet, teeth bared, backs arching.*
>
> *ONE HUNTER!* a wolf shouts in guttural, snarling Common.*
>
> *More wolves pour from the mouth of the cave—two larger ones leading, their fur crackling with unnatural energy. Blue light pulses from one (eyes and veins glow electric). Orange firelight dances across the other's fur.*

**If Party Approaches Stealthily (Won Stealth Check):**

> *You watch the wolves long enough to understand: they're not mindless. The pack is *organized*. While some rest and eat, others maintain the fence. One sits alone, clearly *thinking*. Another paces with agitation.*
>
> *Then another wolf—the one with blue-glowing eyes—notices your shadows at the treeline. It lifts its head.*
>
> *The camp freezes.*
>
> *"Visitors," the blue-eyed wolf says, voice calm and measured. A large gray wolf, scarred and dignified. "Not aggressive yet. Hold positions. I will greet them."*

### The Alpha Pair: Bolt & Flame

**BOLT** (Alpha male, negotiator)
- **Appearance:** Large gray wolf, scarred, blue-glowing eyes and veins (lightning residue)
- **Personality:** Calm, measured, philosophical, formal speaker (recently learned Common)
- **Motivation:** Wants pack's survival through coexistence or relocation; fears war
- **Speech Pattern:** Formal, deliberate, thoughtful: "We were animals once. Now we are more. This brings... complications."

**FLAME** (Alpha female, warmonger)
- **Appearance:** Large scarred she-wolf, orange-glowing eyes, fire-bright marks on fur
- **Personality:** Aggressive, passionate, decisive, defensive
- **Motivation:** Wants pack dominance through strength; fears weakness/surrender
- **Speech Pattern:** Snarling, intense, challenging: "We should *take* what we need! Negotiation is for prey who grovel!"

**Stat Blocks:**

**Bolt (CR 2, 47 HP)**
- **AC** 14 (natural armor) | **HP** 47 (7d10+10) | **Speed** 50 ft
- **STR** 17 (+3) **DEX** 15 (+2) **CON** 15 (+2) **INT** 12 (+1) **WIS** 14 (+2) **CHA** 8 (-1)
- **Skills** Perception +3, Stealth +4 | **Senses** passive Perception 13 | **Languages** Common
- **Challenge** 2 (450 XP)

**Traits:**
- Keen Hearing and Smell (advantage on checks relying on these)
- Pack Tactics (advantage on attack rolls if ally within 5 ft of target)

**Actions:**
- **Bite:** +5 to hit, reach 5 ft, one target. *Hit:* 7 (1d6+3) piercing. Target makes DC 13 Strength save or is knocked prone.
- **Lightning Breath (Recharge 5–6):** 30-foot line. Each creature makes DC 13 Dexterity save, taking 9 (2d8) lightning damage on failed save, half on successful.

- **AC** 14 (natural armor) | **HP** 47 (7d10+10) | **Speed** 50 ft
- **STR** 17 (+3) **DEX** 15 (+2) **CON** 15 (+2) **INT** 12 (+1) **WIS** 14 (+2) **CHA** 8 (-1)
- **Skills** Perception +3, Stealth +4 | **Senses** passive Perception 13 | **Languages** Common

- Keen Hearing and Smell

- **Bite:** +5 to hit, reach 5 ft, one target. *Hit:* 7 (1d6+3) piercing. Target makes DC 13 Strength save or is knocked prone.

**Other Pack Wolves** (6–8 visible, ~3–4 hiding in cave)
- Standard **Wolves** (MM p341) | **AC** 13 | **HP** 5 (1d6+2) | **Speed** 40 ft | **Challenge** 1/8 (25 XP)
- 4 wolves wear crude **leather armor** (+1 AC, total AC 14)

### Combat: Wolf Den Battle

**Only triggered if party attacks immediately or Flame forces combat.**

**Initial Setup:**
- **Party Position:** At cave mouth (150 ft away) OR trees (100 ft away) depending on approach
- **Wolf Position:** Scattered around cave and interior; Bolt and Flame emerge when combat starts
- **Round 1 Combatant Count:** 6 wolves visible; 2 (Bolt & Flame) arrive Round 2

**Combat Tactics:**

**Bolt's Strategy:**
- Leads from front; uses Lightning Breath on groups (careful not to hit allies)
- Fights intelligently but tries to *end combat quickly*
- If 2+ of his wolves die without reciprocal losses, he roars for ceasefire: "This benefits neither of us!"

**Flame's Strategy:**
- Aggressive; uses Fire Breath liberally (doesn't care if wolves catch fire)
- Fights to kill; wants to prove dominance
- Will attack Bolt if she feels he's being "too soft" (around 50% HP, Flame may attack Bolt as well as party)

**Pack Wolves:**
- Use Pack Tactics; try to flank
- Protect young (if party gets near northern sub-chamber, wolves go berserk)
- Fight until Bolt yields or until 50% are dead

#### Scaling Combat by Party Size

| Party Size | Wolf Count | Special Rules | Difficulty |
|------------|-----------|---------------|------------|
| 2 players | 4 wolves + Bolt (NO Flame) | Reduce standard pack; Bolt fights alone to protect pack | Hard |
| 3 players | 6 wolves + Bolt + Flame | Full encounter | Medium |
| 4 players | 8 wolves + Bolt + Flame | Add 2 armored wolves | Medium |
| 5+ players | 10 wolves + Bolt + Flame + armored wolves | Max combat load | Medium |

#### Combat Flow

**Round 1:** Party and wolves roll initiative. 6 wolves act; Bolt/Flame are inside cave (arrive next round).

**Round 2:** Bolt emerges along with 2 additional armored wolves. Flame will follow if combat is intense. Bolt makes assessment: "You fight well. But this ends no victory for either side."

**Critical Choice Point (after Round 2–3):**
- **If 3+ wolves are dead:** Bolt roars for halt. "Hold! This madness serves no purpose!"
- **If Bolt drops below 50% HP:** He yields: "Stop! I yield! We can... talk?"
- **If Flame is challenged:** She doubles down aggression (attacks Bolt if he seems weak)

#### Tactical Notes

**Party Advantages:**
- Humanoid tactics (formations, ranged support)
- Armor and ranged weapons
- Can heal/support (if healing spells available)

**Wolf Advantages:**
- Pack Tactics (flanking bonus)
- Speed (40–50 ft movement vs. 30 ft for most PCs)
- Resistance to certain spells (wolves are feral, harder to affect mentally)
- Breath attacks (area damage)

**Ending Combat (Multiple Pathways):**

1. **Party Kills All Wolves** (pyrrhic victory): All pack wolves die; pups flee or are killed. Bolt may yield at <25% HP ("You've won. Take what you want. Just... let the pups flee."). Flame fights to the death.

2. **Party Forces Stalemate** (negotiation path): Party disarms combat diplomatically. Bolt shouts "Hold!" and Flame is forced to stand down (Bolt's authority over pack is recognized).

3. **Party Is Overrun** (unlikely but possible): DM can rule party wisdom prevails ("We can't win; let's negotiate.") or allow TPK (extremely unlikely in designed adventure—not intended to be lethal).

### Negotiation: The Other Path

**If party chooses negotiation (or forces stalemate in combat):**

**Bolt's Opening Statement:**

> *The blue-eyed wolf, clearly the pack leader, steps forward carefully. His movements are deliberate, non-threatening. He speaks with precision—each word measured and formal.*
>
> *"You understand we are... sentient. Not beasts. This is rare knowledge. It changes what comes next. I am **Bolt**, alpha of this pack. These are my people. We do not wish war with yours."*

**Bolt Explains Their Situation:**

- 3+ weeks ago, a man (Alexi) came through their territory
- The pack attacked to defend their hunting grounds (normal wolf behavior)
- In the fight, "there was a light. A *surge* of magic"
- Alexi's body fell, but before dying, he released magical energy that *changed* the pack
- The wolves woke up with consciousness—memories, language, understanding
- "We went from animals to people in an instant. Suddenly we could think. We could *fear death*. We knew our young mattered. We knew sacrifice."

**Bolt's Proposal:**

**Option A: Relocation**
> "We will leave your lands entirely. Move west into wild country where no humans settle. You will never see us again. In exchange, you do not hunt us to extinction when we are helpless and learning."

**Option B: Territory Treaty**
> "We take the western woods and moorlands for ourselves. You keep your farms. We will kill the Owlbear that plagues you and keep other predators at bay. You let us live without persecution. It is trade—your protection against ours."

**Option C: Surrender (if party demanded it)**
> "We will disperse into smaller groups and move away. We will not return for three seasons. After that... we must find a home somewhere."

**Flame's Counterargument:**
> *The fire-marked she-wolf steps forward, snarling.*
>
> *"You are weak, Bolt! These are predators—they will follow us, hunt us. We should take what we need and fight them for dominance! We are pack! We are STRONG!"*

**The Crack Between Them:**
- Bolt wants coexistence or relocation (peaceful)
- Flame wants dominance through warfare
- Tension between them is *real*
- If party is perceptive (**Insight check DC 12**): "These two leaders disagree fundamentally. Their conflict could destabilize the pack."

### The Featherock Revelation

**If party mentions Featherock the shepherd:**
> *Bolt's eyes dim with something like sorrow.*
>
> *"We did not intend to harm him. When you are newly conscious, you struggle with... what is right. He fought; we defended. We chose to release him rather than kill. It was mercy, by our standards. But I know it was not mercy to him."*

**Consequence:**
- Bolt demonstrates moral capacity (he *regrets* harm)
- Flame scoffs: "Sentiment! Weakness!"
- Shows fundamental difference between leaders

### Council Vote Path (If Negotiation Succeeds)

**If party agrees to present wolf proposal to Welton council:**

**Return to Village:**
- Party reports negotiation to council
- Council convenes for vote

**Council Voting Mechanics:**
- **Father Merriksonn:** GUARANTEED YES (believes wolves deserve chance; his brother's magic caused this)
- **Corel:** LIKELY YES (he's wise and sees wolves as people now)
- **Tillus Merrion:** SWING VOTE (merchant-focused, distrusts anything that disrupts trade)
- **4 Other Council Members:** NEUTRAL / PERSUADABLE

**Meeting the Council:**
Party presents their proposal to 5 council members (Father Merriksonn and Corel already know). Party must convince at least 2 more members to vote YES (need 3+ YES votes total for approval).

**Persuasion Approach:**

| Argument | DC | Swing Vote Effect |
|----------|----|--------------------|
| "The wolves are intelligent and moral—we negotiated peace" | 12 | +1 vote (if you make good case) |
| "Father Merriksonn's brother's magic caused this—they're victims, not monsters" | 15 | +1 vote if priest speaks (he will) |
| "They'll keep predators out of our lands and protect farms" | 13 | +1 economic argument (appeals to Tillus) |
| "If we don't agree, they'll relocate but may cause trouble elsewhere" | 14 | +1 threat avoidance |
| "Killing sentient creatures is morally wrong" | *var.* | Depends on council's alignment |
| Intimidation or threats | *auto fail* | -1 vote (offends council) |

**Vote Outcome:**
- **3+ YES votes:** Treaty passes. Wolves relocate peacefully. Reward paid.
- **Fewer than 3 YES:** Treaty fails. Wolves will leave but may cause future problems. (They keep raiding from distance; consequences later.)

### Alternative Endings: Table of Outcomes

| Outcome | Result | World Change | Future Consequence |
|---------|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Wolves Killed (Total)** | All pack members slain; pups too | Threat eliminated; mystery unsolved | "Whatever caused them is still out there" (Elric notes later) |
| **Negotiated Peace (Treaty)** | Wolves given western territory; treaty signed | Wolves become permanent allies; raiding stops immediately | Bolt helps in future; Flame remains resentful; potential for betrayal |
| **Negotiated Relocation** | Wolves leave Northreach entirely | Threat removed; mystery: "Where did they go?" | Rumors of awakened wolves elsewhere; possible return later |
| **Deception (Claim Victory)** | Party lies to village; secretly lets wolves live | Village believes problem solved; wolves relocated carefully | Wolves raid again later; party's lie exposed; loss of reputation |

### Common Questions for Scene 5

**Q: "What if we try to kill the pups?"**
A: Bolt and Flame will fight to the death to protect them. Violence will be absolute. Party gains nothing and loses morality.

**Q: "Can we negotiate different terms than Bolt offered?"**
A: Yes. Bolt is *open to discussion*. Creative solutions (designated hunting season, shared land, etc.) are negotiable if reasonable.

**Q: "What if we try to make Flame alpha instead?"**
A: Risky. Flame will be grateful but will pursue aggressive policies. Raids will intensify; world becomes darker. Not recommended.

**Q: "How long is this negotiation scene?"**
A: 30–50 minutes depending on roleplay depth. Can compress to 15 min if needed.

### DM Notes

- **Tone:** This is the *heart* of the adventure. Weight the moment. The party is meeting a *people*, not a monster.
- **Emotional Resonance:** Allow players to *feel* the wolves' desperation and Bolt's hope. This makes the choice matter.
- **No "Right" Answer:** Killing, negotiating, deceiving—each has costs and benefits. Player choice should be respected.
- **Future Hooks:** Whichever path is chosen determines consequences in future adventures (Frozen Sick, Temple, etc.)

### Party Size Adjustments: Complete Table

| Party Size | Encounter Scaling | Difficulty | Recommended Changes |

### Specific Encounter Scaling

#### Scene 1: Road Encounter

**2 Players:** 4 Wolves (not 8)
- Fast paced; wolves may flee after 1 round
- Give shepherds +2 more sheepdogs as aid
- Emphasize "you survived; how was that possible?" (foreshadows intelligence)

**3 Players:** 6 Wolves (default)
- Standard difficulty; wolves retreat around round 4

**4–5 Players:** 8–10 Wolves
- Extended combat; add Dire Wolf variant if 5+ players
- Wolves become bolder; may press party harder

#### Scene 4: Owlbear (Optional)

**2 Players:** Skip entirely OR reduce to 30 HP
- Owlbear should not be lethal; threat should be "we can't win; let's avoid it"

**3 Players:** Full 40 HP Owlbear
- Deadly fight; party should consider fleeing

**4+ Players:** 45–50 HP Owlbear
- Tough but winnable; may take 2–3 rounds of combat

#### Scene 5: Wolf Den Combat

**2 Players:** 4 Wolves + Bolt only
- Flame doesn't emerge until Bolt is heavily wounded
- Party has option to flee; not punished for it

**3 Players:** 6 Wolves + Bolt + Flame
- Full as written; medium difficulty

**4–5 Players:** 8 Wolves + Bolt + Flame + 2 Armored Wolves
- Challenging; test party's coordination
- Owlbear scars on wolves emphasize they're dangerous

### Scaling Philosophy for Wolves of Welton

**Core Principle:** "Wolves should be dangerous but not lethal."

This adventure rewards *problem-solving* more than combat skill. Scaling should improve pacing and threat-feeling, not dramatically increase lethality.

**If party is underleveled (Level 0–1):**
- Reduce wolf numbers

- Add wolves, not stronger wolves
- Flesh out negotiation tactics (make Bolt a worthy conversational opponent)

## CHAPTER 8: COMMON QUESTIONS & TIPS

### For New DMs Running Wolves of Welton

**Q: "What if players want to let the wolves go without confrontation?"**
A: That's the negotiation path—embrace it! If party doesn't want to fight, let the diplomatic route play out. Bolt will be grateful; Flame challenge needs to be managed, but it's doable.

**Q: "My party killed some wolves early in combat. Does the pack still exist?"**
A: Yes, absolutely. Bolt and Flame escaped with ~70% of the pack. Even if players kill 5 wolves, thepack of 10+ still exists. Combat power is reduced but not eliminated. They can re-contest or flee.

**Q: "The council vote is a tie. What happens?"**
A: Father Merriksonn and Corel both vote YES if negotiation happened. You need 2 more votes (out of remaining 5). If tied after party arguments (3–3), Corel makes passionate speech: "Intelligent creatures deserve understanding." Tie breaks in favor of treaty. Tillus walks out in protest; vote passes 3–2.

**Q: "Players want to enslave / control the wolves. Should I allow it?"**
A: Not recommended. Bolt and Flame are sapient individuals with agency. Attempting control triggers combat to the death. You can roleplay it, but outcomes should favor freedom. Slavery makes party evil; adjust reputation accordingly if campaign permits evil play.

**Q: "What if party kills Father Merriksonn or an important NPC?"**
A: Consequences follow. Killing Father Merriksonn turns Welton against party (reputation - 5); makes council voting impossible (can't negotiate peace without him). Adventure can still conclude if party commits fully to extermination. Campaign continues with reduced NPC support going forward.

**Q: "How do we keep the mystery feeling fresh when answer comes in Frozen Sick?"**
A: Awesome question. Plant subtle clues in Wolves that make sense once Frozen Sick hits. The Aeorian symbols, the "oldness" of the magic, Elexi's notes about research—these all click into place. Good foreshadowing makes revelation feel satisfying, not cheap.

**Q: "Can party kill Bolt and keep Flame as ally?"**
A: Technically yes, but Flame will *hate* party forever. She'll take "alliance" but will betray at worst moment. Not recommended; creates negative ally. Better to negotiate with Bolt early or just kill whole pack.

### For Experienced DMs: Depth & Variations

**Q: "How can I make the wolves feel more *complex*?"**
A: Give them personalities, backstories, dilemmas. Bolt struggles with leadership (Flame challenges him constantly). What did the wolves dream about before gaining consciousness? Do they miss the simple pleasure of just being beasts? Explore philosophy with intelligent party members.

**Q: "What if I want longer negotiation scenes?"**
A: Excellent. Bolt can offer multiple meeting points (neutral ground, party's camp, inside cave). Spend 2–3 roleplayed scenes with him before final vote. Let Flame's aggression escalate—maybe she attacks Bolt mid-treaty, forcing party to take sides.

**Q: "Can I make Alexi still alive?"**
A: Yes, but changes adventure significantly. If Alexi is found alive in wolf den, he's magically weakened / traumatized by the surge. He can explain EXACTLY what Salsvault is and what he triggered. This shortcuts Frozen Sick's revelation. Good variant for higher-level parties or if you want to accelerate mystery.

**Q: "How do I hint at larger mystery without spoiling Frozen Sick?"**
A: Have Elric mention other phenomena (cold plague, magical accident, prophecies of awakening). Have Alexi's notes hint at "underground facility" and "automatic defense systems still active." Have Bolt describe the magic as "old, older than memory, and *still working.*" Flavor clues without spoiling facts.

### DM Tips for Best Experience

**Set the Tone Early:**
- Read the DM Notes (above) before session 1
- Know who Bolt and Flame *are* (not just stats, but philosophy, fear, hope)
- Understand that this adventure is *moral* not just tactical

**Use Atmosphere:**
- Describe the forest's dread (wolves stalking)
- Emphasize the moment of discovering wolf decorations (realization: they're not mindless)
- Play suspenseful music during negotiation

**Let NPCs Matter:**
- Father Merriksonn's grief about Alexi should feel *real*
- Featherock's trauma should make party hesitant to dismiss any possibility
- Corel's quiet respect should show player that older, wiser folks endorse peace

**Honor Choices:**
- Don't railroad toward negotiation (if party wants to kill, allow it with consequences)
- Don't punish moral choices (killing all wolves is a valid solution; don't make it seem "wrong")
- Let outcomes shape the campaign (wolves alive changes future adventure options)

**Make Combat Cinematic:**
- When wolves are involved, emphasize their *sapience* even in battle
- Have Bolt yell things like "Protect the young!" or "Fall back!" (shows they're strategizing)
- When they start losing, have them *express fear* or *beg for mercy* (heightens moral weight)

**Take Pacing Breaks:**
- After major combat or negotiation, pause for 5 minutes
- Let emotional weight settle
- Ask party: "How are you feeling about what just happened?"
- These moments make adventure *memorable*
