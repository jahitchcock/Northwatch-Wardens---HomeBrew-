## CHAPTER 1: ADVENTURE OVERVIEW

### Campaign Context

**Where This Fits**
- **Campaign Arc:** Arc 1 — Awakening: The Mystery Deepens (Part 1 of 2)
- **Placement:** Early campaign (Levels 1–3), flexible order
- **Follows:** Can stand alone or follow Wolves of Welton
- **Precedes:** Any Arc 2 adventure; excellent intro to dragon politics
- **Recommended Party Size:** 2–5 players, Levels 1–3
- **Tone:** Uplifting but grounded; heroic but humble

### Adventure Premise

*The merchant village of Pinebrook sits on the frontier, far from protective armies and major resources. When a patrol discovers a baby silver dragon, barely alive, frozen in ice, the village faces a moral crisis: investigate the threat that forced the dragon into this state, or tend to the child and risk the village's safety. The party must navigate icy caves, outsmart desperate enemies, and ultimately face the dragon's mother—a powerful entity who could be Pinebrook's greatest ally or its doom.*

### Core Themes

1. **Mercy & Heroism:** Healing the dragon baby is the right choice, even if dangerous.
2. **Frontier Fragility:** Small towns can't defend themselves; they rely on luck and heroes.
3. **Dragons as Individuals:** Dragons aren't just monsters; they're people with families and agency.
4. **Consequences of Indifference:** If no one helps the dragon, Rorn's wrath falls on Pinebrook.

### The Aeorian Echo Connection

*What Players Learn:*
- Magical phenomenon is creating **frozen conditions** in places where no winter exists
- Weather patterns are destabilizing (unusual cold)
- This suggests **regional magical interference** (not just localized events)

*What Remains Hidden:*
- Source of freezing (Salsvault — revealed later)
- Scope of weather change (affects multiple frontier villages)
- Whether the dragon's lair was intentionally targeted or accidentally caught in overflow


### What Players Know (Before Starting)

- A patrol in Pinebrook found something frozen in ice
- The party is asked to help investigate
- Something is causing unusual cold and danger in the area
- A child's life may depend on their actions

### Common Pacing Issues & Solutions

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| **Lair feels like dungeon crawl** | Focus on 3–4 key rooms; skip empty chambers; use skill challenges to speed past non-combat |
| **Combat with living icicles drags** | They have low HP; resolve quickly; emphasize visual spectral quality over mechanics |
| **Party wants to befriend egg snatchers** | Allow negotiation (very unlikely—they're desperate); combat is more rewarding |
| **Rorn feels overpowered** | Establish she won't attack party unless they harm dragon; emphasize gratitude over dominance |
| **Blessing mechanics feel unclear** | Print 1-page card explaining blessing (+1 attack/save for day, once/mission rescuing innocent) |

### Running as a Beginner Adventure

**If party members are new to D&D:**
- Spend time explaining skill checks (DC meaning, die rolls, success/failure)
- Demonstrate advantage/disadvantage with clear examples
- Use the Lair Challenges as teaching moments (climbing, riddles, cold resistance)
- Have Kole or Rorn explain mechanics in-character ("Try to roll high; you need to beat the difficulty")
- Slow pacing; allow questions and table discussion

**If party members are experienced:**
- Speed through mechanics explanations
- Assume knowledge of combat, skill checks, saves
- Focus on roleplay and unique dragon encounter
- Allow complex tactical approaches to lair challenges

### Session Zero Guidance

**Confirm with players before starting:**

- [ ] "This is an **introductory adventure** with a baby dragon at its heart"
- [ ] "Tone: hopeful and heroic, with real danger and moral choices"
- [ ] "You'll meet a patrol, discover a dragon in ice, navigate a magical lair, and ultimately become allies with a dragon"
- [ ] "This adventure emphasizes **mercy and heroism** over aggression"
- [ ] "Skill checks are going to teach you how climbing, persuasion, and ability tests work"
- [ ] "Dragons in this world are intelligent, often noble, and potential allies"

## SCENE 1: THE FROZEN DISCOVERY

**Scene Purpose**
Introduce the adventure, meet key NPCs, establish tone (wonder + investigation), and present the moral crisis that drives the adventure forward.

**Time & Location**
- **Timing:** Daytime in Pinebrook village
- **Location:** Pinebrook (merchant village) or nearby patrol outpost
- **Duration:** 20–30 minutes
- **Atmosphere:** Urgent, cold, beautiful, mysterious

### Read-Aloud Text: The Frozen Mystery

> *You arrive in Pinebrook, a merchant village nestled against low hills. Recently, the weather has turned unusually cold—deeper cold than winter should bring. Frost clings to every surface despite the season being early autumn.*
>
> *Captain Emmajeen Kole, head of the village militia (all 6 of them), meets you with worry etched across her face. It's clear she's been awake all night.*

### Captain Emmajeen Kole (NPC, Commoner/Soldier)

**Appearance:** Human woman in her late 40s, weathered face, kind eyes, militia armor worn well

**Role:** Patrol leader, village protector, experienced but overextended

**Personality:**
- Commanding but not unkind
- Pragmatic; makes hard decisions quickly
- Protective of her village; willing to ask for outside help (sign of competence)
- Trust-building; warmly greets party members by name if introduced

**Kole's Greeting:**

> "Thank you for coming. I'm Captain Kole. We have a situation we can't handle alone. Three days ago, one of our patrols found something frozen in ice about an hour's travel north of here. At first, we thought it was... well, we didn't know what to think. It's a dragon. A baby dragon. Alive, but barely. And something **forced** it into that ice. Something dangerous."

### Kole's Information (What She Knows)

With **DC 10 Insight**, party members understand she's:
- Hiding something (or uncertain) about what put the dragon there
- Genuinely distressed about the village's vulnerability
- Hopeful the party can help

**What Kole reveals if asked directly:**

1. The dragon was found in an **icy cave complex** about 1 hour north of Pinebrook
2. The ice is **unnatural**—thick, hard, and cold enough to harm unprotected travelers
3. The baby dragon was trapped inside the ice, barely breathing
4. A **larger dragon** (adult-sized) has been circling the area for two days, making distressed calls
5. The village **cannot defend** against an adult dragon
6. There are **humanoid tracks** near the lair—someone or something else is involved

### Kole's Dilemma (The Moral Question)

> "We have two choices, and they're both terrible. One: we leave the baby dragon where it is. Eventually, the mother dragon will get desperate or angry. Pinebrook is small—we can't stop a dragon. She'll destroy us when she decides we're the problem. Two: we try to help the baby. But that means going into that lair, facing whatever put it there in the first place, and hoping we don't start a war with the mother dragon. I don't have soldiers strong enough to do this alone. That's why I'm asking you."

### The Path Forward

**If party agrees to help:**
- Kole provides provisioning (cold-weather gear, rope, torches; 3 days' rations)
- She assigns one militia member to guide them (describe as nervous but competent)
- Provides rough map of lair entrance and cave layout
- Warns them: "There are tracks of other creatures in there—not just the dragon. Be careful."

**If party hesitates:**
- Kole makes a personal plea: "That baby is innocent. Whatever happens next, we can't let an innocent die if we have the power to stop it."
- Offers increased reward (200 gp instead of 100 gp if baby is saved)

**If party refuses:**
- Kole thanks them but looks devastated
- Consequence: Adult dragon, desperate and angry, attacks Pinebrook in 2–3 days
- Party can later receive a desperate messenger asking for help (alternative entry point)

### Transition to Scene 2

Once committed to helping:

> *Kole leads you north out of Pinebrook. As you travel, the temperature drops steadily. The landscape becomes increasingly strange—frost on leaves, ice on normally-flowing streams, unnatural cold despite the season.*
>
> *After an hour of travel, you see it: a cave mouth ringed with jagged icicles. Even from a distance, you can see unnatural ice formations. And from within the cave, you hear the faint, distressed sound of something small crying—a dragon hatchling.*

## SCENE 2: THE LAIR APPROACH & CHALLENGES

**Scene Purpose**
Introduce the lair environment, present skill-based challenges (climbing, puzzle-solving), establish the icy hazard theme, and build toward the baby dragon discovery.

**Time & Location**
- **Timing:** Immediate (moments after traveling)
- **Location:** Cave entrance and first two chambers
- **Duration:** 30–45 minutes
- **Atmosphere:** Cold, magical, beautiful but dangerous, wonder mixed with dread

### Read-Aloud Text: The Lair Entrance

> *The cave entrance is a massive maw ringed with hanging icicles the size of swords. Inside, everything is coated in thick ice—walls, ceiling, floor—all glowing with a faint blue-white light that seems to emanate from the ice itself.*
>
> *The sound of the dragon hatchling grows louder as you approach: a keening cry, like a child in distress.*
>
> *Before you lies the challenging part: the entrance chamber's floor is pure ice, sloping downward at a 45-degree angle into darkness. Handholds are scarce. The cold is so intense that exposed skin tingles.*

### Environmental Hazard: Extreme Cold

**Unprotected Exposure:**
Each minute in the lair without cold protection (via spell, hot drink, magic item, or natural resistance):
- Make **DC 10 Constitution save**
- Success: No damage
- Failure: 1d6 cold damage

**Provided by Kole:** Cold-weather gear (reduces DC to 12) and warm drinks (grants advantage on save for 10 minutes)

### Lair Chamber 1: The Descent

**Skill Challenge: Navigating the Icy Slope**

Party must cross a 60-foot icy slope to reach the middle chamber. **DC 12 Acrobatics or Dexterity (Ice Skating equivalent)**

**Success (2 consecutive wins):** Cross without incident; reach middle chamber safely

**Failure (2 consecutive failures):** Slip and slide 10 feet down the slope; take 1d6 damage; must start over

**Alternative approaches (allow creativity):**
- **Climbing:** DC 13 (handholds are slippery and few)
- **Magic (Grease, Heat):** Auto-success but may trigger avalanche
- **Rope/Climbing Gear:** Advantage on Acrobatics, auto-success if secured
- **Strength (brute force):** Very difficult (DC 16), not recommended

**Scaling by Party Size:**
- **2 players:** Only 1 success needed; easier slope
- **3 players:** 2 successes required; standard difficulty
- **4+ players:** 2 successes required; but more opportunities for helping

### Lair Chamber 2: The Frozen Ritual Vault

**Read-Aloud Text:**

> *The slope opens into a vast chamber—60 feet wide, 40 feet high, ceiling lost in shadow. The floor is covered in ritualistic ice formations: concentric circles of frozen runes, radial pathways of sculpted ice, and in the center of the chamber, suspended within a pillar of solid ice, is the **baby dragon**.*
>
> *The creature is small—perhaps 10 feet long—with silver scales that shimmer even through the ice. It's curled in a fetal position, wings wrapped around its tiny body, and its cries seem to echo from afar.*
>
> *But the ritual isn't empty. At the outer circle of the runes, you see movement: three large, humanoid creatures made entirely of living ice, standing motionless as if on guard.*

### Encounter: Living Icicles

**The Living Icicles** (x3, Elemental creatures)
- **AC** 10 | **HP** 7 (2d6) each | **Speed** 30 ft
- **STR** 14 (+2) | **DEX** 8 (−1) | **CON** 14 (+2) | **INT** 3 (−4) | **WIS** 10 (+0) | **CHA** 3 (−4)
- **Damage Immunities** Cold
- **Damage Vulnerabilities** Fire (takes double damage from fire-based attacks)
- **Senses** Darkvision 60 ft, Passive Perception 10
- **Challenge** 1/8 (25 XP each)

**Special Trait — Fragile Composition:**
If a Living Icicle takes fire damage, it immediately takes 5 additional damage and its AC becomes 8 for the next round (partially melted, more vulnerable).

**Actions:**
- **Fist:** +4 to hit | 1d8+2 bludgeoning damage | Creature pushed 5 feet on hit
- **Breath of Frost (Recharge 5–6):** 15-foot cone, targets within must make **DC 12 Dexterity save** or take 1d6 cold damage

### Approach Options

**Option A: Combat**
- Party fights the three Living Icicles
- Straightforward but time-consuming
- Emphasize that fire is effective; ice melts when threatened
- Ice creatures are unintelligent; they don't flee or negotiate

**Option B: Stealth & Bypass**
- **DC 13 Stealth:** Move through the chamber undetected
- If successful: reach the dragon pillar without combat
- If failed: combat begins

**Option C: Magic or Distraction**
- Heat spells (Burning Hands, Fire Bolt) create steam and distraction
- Ice creatures are drawn toward heat source
- Creative magic solutions auto-succeed

**Option D: Social (Unusual)**
- **Very** difficult to negotiate with mindless ice
- Only works if party can convince them they're not a threat (near-impossible, DC 16+)
- Not recommended path

### Scaling by Party Size

| Party Size | Living Icicle Count | Additional Hazard |
|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| 2 players | 1 icicle | Party can bypass with stealth (DC 12) |
| 3 players | 2 icicles | Or one 3-icicle combat at reduced HP (5 HP each) |
| 4 players | 3 icicles (standard) | Standard encounter |
| 5+ players | 3 icicles + 1 ice elemental | Enhanced challenge |

### The Dragon Pillar

Once Living Icicles are bypassed or defeated, party reaches the ice pillar containing the baby dragon.

**Mechanics to Free the Dragon:**

- **Option 1: Brute Force (Strength-based)**
  - **DC 14 Strength check** to break pillar
  - Success: Pillar cracks; dragon falls gently (6 feet) to ground (no damage)
  - Failure: Pillar holds; try again

- **Option 2: Heat (Fire-based)**
  - Cast fire spell or use torch to melt ice
  - **DC 10 Intelligence (Arcana)** to melt without harming dragon
  - Success: Ice melts safely; dragon slides free
  - Failure: Takes 1d4 fire damage itself (still survives)

- **Option 3: Magic (Dispel Magic)**
  - **DC 12 Intelligence (Arcana)**
  - Success: Automatic safe release
  - Failure: No effect; try other method

- **Option 4: Tools or Leverage**
  - Climbers' tools, pitons, or rope
  - **DC 11 Dexterity** to carefully free dragon
  - Success: Safe extraction

### Freeing the Dragon: The Moment

> *As the ice gives way, the baby dragon tumbles out with a surprised cry. It's no larger than a horse—silver scales catching the ice-light, tiny wings still damp from the ice. It looks up at you with eyes full of wonder and fear.*
>
> *"M-mother?" it mews, voice small and confused. "Are you... my rescuer?"*

### Nytha, the Baby Silver Dragon

**Name:** Nytha (young female dragon)

**Appearance:** Silver scales, delicate wings, large innocent eyes, about 10 feet long

**Personality:**
- Child-like innocence and curiosity
- Grateful to rescuers; bond-forming quickly
- Confused and frightened
- Speaks Common (dragons are naturally intelligent)

**What Nytha knows:**
1. She was **playing** in and around her mother's lair
2. A **blizzard of unnatural ice** suddenly swept in
3. She was **trapped** in the pillar without understanding why
4. The cold was **painful** but she couldn't escape
5. She's been calling for her mother but can't hear a response
6. There were **humanoid creatures** in the lair before the ice came (they may still be here)

**Nytha's Immediate Needs:**
- Warmth (body heat, blankets, or heat spells help)
- Reassurance (she's traumatized but trusting of rescuers)
- Protection (she cannot fight; she's helpless in combat)
- Her mother (greatest priority; she's desperate to reunion)

### Egg Discovery (Optional Lore)

If party searches the pillar's base or surrounding area:
- **DC 11 Perception:** finds scales and burn marks
- Hidden beneath another ice formation: **a dragon egg, unattended and cracked**
- The egg is dead (no hatchling); it's old and frosted over
- **Implication:** Nytha had a sibling that didn't survive (adds emotional weight to rescue)

## SCENE 3: EGG SNATCHERS & COMBAT

**Scene Purpose**
Final combat encounter before reaching Rorn; dramatic rescue scenario; introduce the antagonists (desperate humans, not evil); scale combat for party level.

**Time & Location**
- **Timing:** While party is comforting Nytha or beginning to leave the chamber
- **Location:** Lower lair chamber (leads to Rorn's main chamber)
- **Duration:** 20–30 minutes
- **Atmosphere:** Urgent, dramatic, moral complexity (enemies are desperate, not evil)

### Read-Aloud Text: The Egg Snatchers Arrive

> *As you prepare to ascend with Nytha, a commotion echoes from deeper in the cave. Voices—human voices, angry and urgent. They're searching for something.*
>
> *"The egg must be here somewhere! The client will pay 500 gold pieces for a dragon egg. We need that money. We need it!"*
>
> *Three figures emerge from the lower chambers: hardened mercenaries, weathered and desperate-looking. They freeze when they see you—and the baby dragon.*
>
> *Their hands immediately move to weapons. They've clearly come to steal the dragon egg.*

### The Egg Snatchers (Mercenary Thugs)

**Egg Snatchers** (x2–3, Human Thugs, CR 1/8 each)
- **AC** 12 (leather armor) | **HP** 18 (4d8) each | **Speed** 30 ft
- **STR** 14 (+2) | **DEX** 12 (+1) | **CON** 12 (+1) | **INT** 10 (+0) | **WIS** 10 (+0) | **CHA** 10 (+0)
- **Skills** Deception +2, Intimidation +2
- **Senses** Passive Perception 10
- **Languages** Common
- **Challenge** 1/8 (25 XP each)

**Special Feature — Pack Tactics:**
Gain advantage on melee attack if ally is within 5 feet of target.

**Actions:**
- **Shortsword or Dagger:** +4 to hit | 1d6+2 slashing | 10-foot range with dagger
- **Intimidate:** Attempt to scare foes; DC 12 Wisdom save or disadvantage on next attack

### The Snatchers' Motivation

**Not Evil, But Desperate:**

These aren't cackling villains. They're mercenaries hired to steal the egg. **If intimidated or talked to:**

> "Look, we're just trying to survive. A client—some wizard in the city—offered 500 gold pieces for a dragon egg. That's enough to buy land, start a farm, feed our families for a year. We don't want trouble. We just want the egg and to be on our way."

**Negotiation DC:**
- **DC 12 Persuasion or Deception:** Convince them to leave peacefully (offer alternative pay; claim the egg is dead; bluff about Rorn)
- **DC 13 Intimidation:** Frighten them away (suggest party is stronger)
- **Combat (alternative):** If they won't back down, defeat them

### Combat (If It Happens)

**Snatchers' Tactics:**
- Attack the party, trying to grab Nytha or push past to search for the egg
- Use **Pack Tactics** to gain advantage when flanking
- Flee when reduced to 5 HP or fewer (self-preservation)
- Will **surrender** if outnumbered or outmatched

**Scaling by Party Size:**

| Party Size | Snatcher Count | Tactics | Difficulty |
|-----------|----------------|---------|-----------|
| 2 players | 1 snatcher | Solo, defensive | Easy |
| 3 players | 2 snatchers | Pack tactics | Medium |
| 4 players | 3 snatchers | Coordinated, flanking | Medium |
| 5+ players | 3 snatchers + 1 guard (AC 13, HP 13) | Organized resistance | Medium |

### Aftermath: Captured or Fleeing Snatchers

**If party defeats or intimidates snatchers:**
- They flee, promising to report to their client (potential future antagonist hook)
- Alternatively: party can capture them and bring them back to Pinebrook (law/justice consequence)
- Snatchers drop: 50 gp each, tools, bedroll, rations

**If party kills all snatchers:**
- Nytha is distressed: "Why did you hurt them? They were just trying to survive..."
- (Moral moment; party reflects on necessity vs. mercy)

## SCENE 4: THE MOTHER DRAGON & REUNION

**Scene Purpose**
Climactic scene; meet Rorn (adult silver dragon), dangerous negotiation encounter, reward players for their heroism, establish dragon as campaign ally.

**Time & Location**
- **Timing:** Immediately after snatchers are dealt with
- **Location:** Lower lair chamber (Rorn's main chamber)
- **Duration:** 20–30 minutes
- **Atmosphere:** Awe, danger, hope, regal beauty

### Read-Aloud Text: The Mother Dragon

> *You follow the passage downward, Nytha at your side. The cave opens into a vast chamber—easily 100 feet across, ceiling lost in shadows high above. The walls are lined with alcoves, treasures, and ancient carvings.*
>
> *And filling this chamber is a **dragon**.*
>
> *She is colossal—60 feet of silver scales, gleaming like starlight. Her wings are folded against her back. Her eyes are ancient, intelligent, and currently fixed directly on you. Ice crystals hang from her horns and spines.*
>
> *When she sees Nytha, she makes a sound—not a roar, but something between a song and a cry. It's a sound full of anguish and hope.*

### Rorn/Hysvearorn (Adult Silver Dragon)

**Rorn** (Ancient Silver Dragon, CR 10+, but **non-hostile**)

**Appearance:** 60-foot silver dragon, regal bearing, ancient dignified air, clearly protective and grieving

**Personality:**
- Protective mother (primary motivator)
- Honorable and regal; respects heroism
- Grateful for rescuing her child
- Dangerous if provoked (but won't attack first)

**Stats (For Reference):**
- **AC** 19 | **HP** 210 (20d20+40) | **Speed** 40 ft, fly 80 ft
- **STR** 23 (+6) | **DEX** 10 (+0) | **CON** 21 (+5) | **INT** 18 (+4) | **WIS** 16 (+3) | **CHA** 18 (+4)
- **Saving Throws** DEX +5, CON +10, WIS +8, CHA +9
- **Damage Resistances** Cold
- **Senses** Truesight 120 ft, blind beyond 120 ft
- **Languages** Common, Elvish, Draconic, and others
- **Challenge** 16 (15,000 XP)
- **Legendary Resistance (3/Day):** Can reroll failed saves

**Important Note:** Rorn will **NOT attack** unless the party harms Nytha or presents a direct threat. She's intelligent enough to understand gratitude.

### The Reunion

When Rorn sees Nytha:

> *"Nytha!"*
>
> *The word is a roar and a song combined. The mother dragon moves toward her child with surprising gentleness, lowering her massive head to nuzzle the baby dragon. Nytha rushes forward, mewing with relief and joy. They touch scaled cheeks in what is clearly a profound moment of reunion.*
>
> *After a long moment, Rorn's ancient eyes focus on you. She bows her head—actually **bows**, a gesture of respect.*

### Speaking with Rorn

Rorn's speech is **ancient, formal, and deeply grateful:**

> "I am Rorn, called Hysvearorn in the old tongue. I was trapped in this lair by unnatural ice—magic that froze even my flames. I could not escape to protect my daughter. But you have done what I could not. You have saved Nytha from a fate of cold and despair. For this, you have my eternal gratitude."

**Topics Rorn will discuss (if asked):**

**1. "What trapped you and Nytha in ice?"**
- Response: "A force of magic—ancient and cold. It came from the north, from a place my kind remembers as defiled. The ice came without warning, without mercy. It is not natural. Something has awakened beneath the earth."
- **Implication:** Aeorian Echo / Salsvault (campaign mystery)

**2. "What happened to the egg?"**
- Response (sorrowful): "My firstborn. Lost to the cold before the barrier formed. Nytha was my second; fortune blessed me that she survived."
- **Implication:** The magical interference is destructive

**3. "Will you attack Pinebrook?"**
- Response: "Why would I attack those who saved my daughter? I am no monster. Your people showed mercy when they had no obligation to. That mercy will be remembered."

### Bahamut's Blessing

After discussion, Rorn makes a formal gesture of gratitude:

> *Rorn's eyes glow with divine light. She lowers her head and touches her horn gently to your forehead—each of you.*
>
> *You feel warmth, peace, and a sense of being blessed by divine forces.*

**Bahamut's Blessing (Granted to all party members):**

- **Effect:** For the next 24 hours, you have:
  - **+1 bonus** to all attack rolls and saving throws
  - **Advantage** on skill checks related to protecting innocents, being merciful, or defending the helpless
  - **Healing** — if you are reduced to 0 HP, you regain 5 HP instead (one use per creature per day)

- **Duration:** 24 hours from blessing (can extend if party visits Rorn again or performs heroic merciful acts)

- **Scaling for repeated use:** If party rescues additional innocents from the Echo corruption, the blessing can be renewed or extended (campaign-long benefit)

### Treasure & Reward

**From Rorn Directly:**
- 100 gp in gems and coins (payment for rescue)
- A **silver scale** from Rorn's own body (can be crafted into armor, jewelry, or kept as memento; treated as uncommon magic item worth 250+ gp)

**From Pinebrook (Kole's reward):**
- 100 additional gp
- **Citizenship in Pinebrook** (can resupply, rest, and use town resources freely)

## CHAPTER 8: COMMON QUESTIONS & TIPS

### For New DMs

**Q: "How do I make sure players understand the moral weight of the adventure?"**
A: Play Kole with genuine concern and Nytha with innocent vulnerability. When players make choices, describe consequences (Nytha's relief vs. fear, Rorn's gratitude vs. wariness). Let emotion guide the narrative.

**Q: "What if players want to kill the dragon instead of helping?"**
A: Allow it, but describe the catastrophic consequences. Rorn will defend her child to the death. Adult dragons are CR 10+ creatures; unless party is high-level, this ends badly for them. Make it clear hostility toward Nytha is a mistake.

**Q: "The lair challenges feel too easy/hard. How do I adjust?"**
A: Skill checks are customizable DCs. For easier: reduce DC by 2–3. For harder: increase by 2–3. Or add additional hazards (multiple icicles approaching, unstable ceiling, time pressure).

**Q: "What if players want to adopt Nytha?"**
A: Lovely sentiment, but Nytha belongs with her mother. However: Rorn might allow brief visits or future travel with Nytha (party guards her during journeys). This creates ongoing campaign tie-in.

**Q: "Can Rorn help us fight future enemies?"**
A: Not every time—dragons have their own concerns. But for **major threats to Pinebrook or serious crises**, Rorn will intervene. Make it special and rare. **Once per campaign maximum** for a direct dragon combat intervention.

### For Experienced DMs: Depth & Variations

**Q: "How can I make Rorn feel like a genuine ancient being, not just a power fantasy?"**
A: Give her alien perspectives. She might not understand human economy, morality, or urgency. She speaks in metaphor and ancient references. She's grateful but also proud—she doesn't take orders; she takes requests.

**Q: "Can I make the Egg Snatchers into recurring characters?"**
A: Absolutely. If they escape, they could become: mercenary contacts, moral dilemmas in future adventures, or antagonists working for a larger villain. Use them to explore moral ambiguity.

**Q: "What if the party wants to find the source of the ice?"**
A: Perfect segue into Frozen Sick or other Echo-hunting adventures. Give them clues: "The ice came from the north, carried on a wind from somewhere deep and ancient." Let curiosity guide them toward the next adventure.

## APPENDIX B: RUNNING THE ADVENTURE FOR BEGINNER PLAYERS

### Brief DM Guidance for New Players

**If your party is completely new to D&D**, use the scenes in this adventure to **teach core mechanics**:

#### Skill Checks (Scene 2: Icy Slope)

> *"Everyone needs to make an Acrobatics check to cross the ice safely. That means you'll roll a d20 and add your Dexterity modifier. Your character sheet should have that number next to Dexterity. The DC is 12—if you roll 12 or higher, you succeed. If you roll lower, you slip."*

**Demonstrate with the first party member:**
1. They roll d20 (show them the die)
2. Ask them to add their modifier (show them the math: "You rolled an 8, your modifier is +1, so 8+1 = 9")
3. Compare to DC: "You needed 12, but you got 9, so you slip 10 feet down the slope."

#### Advantage & Disadvantage (Example in Lair Challenges)

> *"You're trying to climb the icy wall, but you're using a rope to help. That gives you advantage—which means you roll the d20 twice and take the higher number."*

#### Combat (Living Icicles, Scene 2)

> *"The ice creatures are attacking. In combat, you roll initiative—a d20 with your Dexterity modifier. Whoever rolls highest acts first. Then you take turns. On your turn, you can move and take an action (like attacking). Keep going until all enemies are defeated or fleeing."*

**For the first attack:**
1. Have the player roll to hit: "Roll d20 and add your attack bonus (you can find that next to your weapon)"
2. Compare to the Icicle's AC: "You need to roll at least a 10, and you rolled a 14, so you HIT!"
3. Ask them to roll damage: "Roll the damage die (d8 for your sword) and add your modifier."

#### Healing & Blessing (After Rorn)

> *"Rorn gives you all a blessing—a gift of magical power. For the next 24 hours, you get +1 to all attack rolls and saving throws. That means every time you attack or make a save, you add an extra point."*

### Teaching Moments (Emphasis)

| Moment | Teaching Point | How to Run It |
|--------|--------------|-----------|
| Icy slope descent | Skill checks and consequences | Walk through the first roll fully; let others roll faster |
| Flying swords appear | Combat mechanics | Use a visual tracker on paper or whiteboard |
| Bahamut's Blessing | Spell/magical effects | Explain bonus clearly; announce it every time it's used |
| Speaking to Rorn | Roleplaying NPCs | Use different voice; ask players what they ask the dragon |

### Encouraging Player Engagement

- **Ask open questions:** "What does your character do when they see the baby dragon?" (allows creative solutions)
- **Celebrate success:** "Your d20 roll of 18 is amazing! You crit and deal double damage!"
- **Make failure matter but not derail:** "You slip down 10 feet—no damage, but you have to climb back up. Try again!"
- **Show NPC reactions:** When players are kind, have Kole/Nytha thank them; when harsh, show sadness
