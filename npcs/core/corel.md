## Profile

![Corel](/portraits/Corel.jpg) {width:130px,float:right,margin:"0 0 10px 15px"}

**Appearance:** A weathered older man with hands that look like they were made for harder work than they are getting, and eyes that have spent forty years reading weather and animal behavior.

**Voice:** Patient and observational — he describes things the way a man does who has learned that the right detail matters more than a lot of words.

**Signature line:** "That's not how a wolf acts. I know how wolves act. Whatever's doing this — it knows how a wolf acts too."

## Knows / Wants / Secret

- **Knows:** Wolf pack behavior, territory patterns, and the specific anomalies in the current attacks that don't fit any natural explanation; the history of the Welton area's relationship with the local wolf packs; who in the village will push for extermination and who can be reasoned with.
- **Wants:** To understand what is happening before anyone does something irreversible. He is the party's strongest natural ally for a diplomatic solution.
- **Secret:** Corel has seen something he hasn't told the village council — a wolf that stopped, looked directly at him, and then deliberately turned away from the flock rather than attacking. He doesn't know what to make of it, but it's the reason he hasn't written off the possibility of a non-violent resolution.

## Season 1: Wolves of Welton — Diplomatic Anchor

Corel is the party's most reliable local ally and the clearest voice for investigating before acting. He can provide invaluable tactical context about wolf behavior, which makes the anomalies more legible. He will push back firmly on any plan that involves wholesale slaughter of the wolves.

Use him as the moral and practical center of the Welton community scenes. He is trusted by nearly everyone in the village except Tillus Merrion, with whom he has a long-running and unresolved argument about the value of things that don't have a price.

If Bolt makes contact with the party, Corel is the first human they should tell — and his reaction (stunned, then quickly analytical) models the response the party should be looking for.
