## Profile

![Ariodh Highwhirl](/portraits/AriodhHighwhirl.png) {width:130px,float:right,margin:"0 0 10px 15px"}

**Appearance:** A slight elf with a stillness to them that is slightly uncanny in a crowded room — they never seem to be in the way and never seem startled.

**Voice:** Quiet and deliberate, with a habit of rephrasing what you just said before responding, as if checking they understood correctly.

**Signature line:** "Before we consider what we can do — what have we not yet tried that doesn't require breaking something?"

## Knows / Wants / Secret

- **Knows:** Several meditation and calming techniques that work on a surprising range of intelligent creatures; a thorough knowledge of non-violent conflict resolution approaches; the philosophical traditions of three or four monastic orders with very different views on when force is justified.
- **Wants:** To resolve conflict at the point before violence becomes the only option. They are not naive about force — they are extremely capable of it — but they find it a failure state, not a solution.
- **Secret:** They left their monastic order after refusing to participate in what they considered an unjust but officially sanctioned act of violence. The order considers them a deserter; the designation hasn't been formally pursued, but if someone with connections to that order shows up in Northreach, there could be complications.

## Season 1: Diplomatic Voice & Combat Specialist

Ariodh is the party's best argument for slowing down. Use them to present the case for talking before fighting, especially in situations involving the wolves of Welton or other intelligent nonhuman NPCs where the party's default instinct might be violence.

They are also quietly one of the most dangerous members of the Wardens in direct combat — their reluctance to fight should make it more notable when they do. A scene where Ariodh decides that force is now justified is a signal that things have gone past the point of no return.

Their calm manner can read as aloof; the party may need to find the right moment to realize they are genuinely warm.
