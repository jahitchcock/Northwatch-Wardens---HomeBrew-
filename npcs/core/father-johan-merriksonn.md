## Profile

![Father Johan Merriksonn](/portraits/FatherJohanMerriksonn.jpg) {width:130px,float:right,margin:"0 0 10px 15px"}

**Appearance:** A broad-shouldered older man with a priest's robe that fits badly and the calloused hands of someone who has always done his own labor; grief has put lines around his eyes that weren't there a year ago.

**Voice:** Steady and practiced in the cadences of comfort, but the steadiness costs him something now that his own family is part of the crisis.

**Signature line:** "Alexi was — is — a good person. Whatever happened, whatever he did, that's still true. I need you to hold onto that while you're looking."

## Knows / Wants / Secret

- **Knows:** The full history of Welton's community, its feuds and its faith; the last known movements of his child Alexi before the disappearance; the spiritual and emotional state of the village with devastating accuracy.
- **Wants:** Alexi found and brought home — alive, or at minimum with an answer he can grieve and build on. He is holding the community together partly through sheer will and will not admit how close he is to breaking.
- **Secret:** He knows that Alexi had been experimenting with sorcery for over a year before the incident — he discovered it and said nothing, hoping it would resolve itself. He blames himself for the current situation and cannot say so, because saying it would mean stepping down as the moral authority the village needs him to be right now.

## Season 1: Wolves of Welton — Grief and Duty

Johan is the emotional center of the Welton arc. His dual role — grieving parent, community moral anchor — creates productive tension. He will give the party everything he knows, but some of what he knows he won't volunteer without the right questions.

He can serve as a pressure point: if the party handles the wolves with callousness, his disappointment is a meaningful cost. If they find a solution that doesn't require wholesale destruction, his relief is a real moment.

The secret about Alexi's sorcery is a confession waiting for the right conversation. If the party creates a safe enough space, he tells them — and it changes what they're looking for.
