## Profile

![Aurixean Valignaak](/portraits/AurixeanValignaak.png) {width:130px,float:right,margin:"0 0 10px 15px"}

**Appearance:** A gold-scaled dragonborn who enters a room like he expects it to be waiting for him, with a voice that carries whether he wants it to or not.

**Voice:** Resonant and deliberate, pitched for an audience even when the audience is two people.

**Signature line:** "I have considered the alternatives. There are none. This is what we do."

## Knows / Wants / Secret

- **Knows:** A broad range of destructive and utility sorcery; the political landscape of dragonborn communities in the broader region (which he navigates carefully); how to read a room and shift the energy of it with minimal effort.
- **Wants:** To matter — to be part of something that history remembers. He is not merely vain; the desire is deeper than that, rooted in a lineage that always expected more from him than he felt equipped to deliver.
- **Secret:** His sorcerous power is larger than he lets on and intermittently harder to control than he admits. He had an incident two years ago that he buried. The suppression is starting to show cracks.

## Season 1: Scene-Dominating Warden

Aurixean fills every room he is in, which is both an asset and a challenge for the party. He is not a villain and not a rival — he is a fellow warden who simply operates on a larger scale than most people around him. Use him to signal that the Guild contains genuine power, not just capable fighters.

He is useful in social situations (his presence commands attention) and in combat (his output is significant). His value for the DM is tonal: scenes with Aurixean tend to feel more consequential.

The control issue is a slow-burn. At DM's discretion, one moment in Season 1 should show a hint of it — something just slightly too large, just slightly too hard to stop.

## Season 2+: Unstable Asset

The cracks in his control become harder to ignore. He may confide in the party — or they may witness something he can't explain away. His arc is about learning that the performance of confidence is not the same as actual stability.
