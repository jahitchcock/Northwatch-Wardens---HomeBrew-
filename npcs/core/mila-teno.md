## Profile

![Mila Teno](/portraits/MilaTeno.png) {width:130px,float:right,margin:"0 0 10px 15px"}

**Appearance:** A young human woman, late teens, with the bearing of someone who decided to stand up straight and is actively concentrating on it.

**Voice:** Earnest and slightly too loud — she hasn't yet learned the warden habit of speaking quietly near anything that might be listening.

**Signature line:** "I can do it. I know you're going to say I'm not ready, but I — I just need someone to tell me what ready actually looks like."

## Knows / Wants / Secret

- **Knows:** The textbook version of field procedure; enough about the local terrain to navigate without getting lost; the names and reputations of every warden at the Waystone, studied like a roster she wants to earn her place on.
- **Wants:** To be taken seriously as a warden, not managed as a rookie. She knows she is inexperienced. She doesn't want to be reminded of it constantly.
- **Secret:** She froze on her first real mission — just for a moment, but long enough for someone else to have been hurt. No one died. She has not told anyone, and she replays it constantly.

## Season 1: Human Cost & Hard Choice NPC

Mila is most valuable in scenes that explore what the Guild's work actually costs the people who do it. Her rookie status lets the party see familiar situations through fresh eyes — and her reaction to the brutal or morally complex things that happen on missions is often more honest than what the veterans show.

Use her for moments where the party has to decide whether to shield her from something or let her face it. Let her be brave and shaken simultaneously.

She is not comic relief and should not be played as incompetent — she is capable, just unfinished. The distinction matters.

Her freeze moment can surface as a plot beat at any point the DM chooses.

## Season 2+: Hardened Scout

She has enough missions under her belt now that the rawness has been replaced by something more complicated. She still cares more visibly than the veterans, but it no longer reads as inexperience — it reads as a choice.
