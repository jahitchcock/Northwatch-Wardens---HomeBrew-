## Profile

**Appearance:** A large tawny wolf with a burn scar along one shoulder that has never fully healed; she moves with deliberate, contained energy that looks like it is always about two seconds from becoming something else.

**Voice:** Fluent, cutting, and impatient — she has mastered language faster than Bolt and uses it like a tool for winning arguments.

**Signature line:** "Bolt wants to negotiate. I want to survive. Those are not the same thing, and you should think carefully about which one you're helping."

## Knows / Wants / Secret

- **Knows:** Exactly how vulnerable the wolf pack is if the humans decide on extermination; the boundaries of the humans' courage and organization; how to use the new intelligence as a weapon rather than a bridge.
- **Wants:** Dominance — not cruelty for its own sake, but genuine safety for the pack through a position of power that humans cannot easily challenge. She believes Bolt's diplomacy will get them killed.
- **Secret:** She is afraid. Specifically, she is afraid of what she is becoming — the hunger for control is not a natural wolf instinct, and some part of her knows it. She does not examine this closely. She is also aware that the awakening changed her in ways she does not fully understand, and that Bolt's stability may be evidence of something she lacks.

## Season 1: Wolves of Welton — Rival Faction Leader

Flame is not a villain, but she is an obstacle to the arc's diplomatic resolution and a genuine threat if the party dismisses her perspective. She needs to be taken seriously as an intelligent creature with a legitimate (if extreme) strategic position.

The party can engage her directly: she is willing to negotiate from a position of respect. She will not negotiate from a position of supplication. If the party approaches her with the same condescension they might bring to a monster encounter, she will not cooperate.

Her fear is a potential key — not to make her sympathetic at the cost of her edge, but to give the party a way to talk to the part of her that knows Bolt might be right.

## Season 2+: Faction Leader or Complicated Antagonist

If the Welton arc gave Flame what she wanted, she is a powerful regional faction leader the party has to deal with as near-equals. If the arc excluded her, she is a recurring antagonist with a grudge and the intelligence to act on it in unexpected ways.
