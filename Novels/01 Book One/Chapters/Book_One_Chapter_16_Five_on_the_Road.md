# The Old Songs of Aevoria
## Book One — *She Who Would Not Be Silent*
### Chapter Sixteen — Five on the Road

---

Maelis caught up with us on the second day out of Thistlebrook. He came up behind us on the track, bow on his back, pack on his shoulders, his face set in the expression of a man doing something he had already decided he would do, with or without our leave.

Wren saw him first. She turned. She smiled.

"Maelis. What are you doing here."

He looked at her. He looked at the road. "I'm coming with you."

"Why."

"Because you're walking into the wild miles. Because they're dangerous. Because you need someone who can hunt, and trap, and shoot. And that's what I do."

She looked at me. Then Brannoc. Then Corwin. Then back to him.

"All right. Come."

He came.

---

I recognized him. Not his face — I had never seen it before. I recognized the place he was standing. Outside the center of a love story. Loving someone who loved someone else.

He looked at me. I looked at him. We said nothing. Neither of us needed to. An unspoken kinship passed between us — the kinship of two people standing in the same place, doing the same thing, and knowing the shape of the other's silence because it was the shape of their own.

We walked. We did not speak of it.

---

The pack hit us on the fourth night.

We were camped in a hollow. The fire was low. Brannoc slept. Wren tended the flames. Corwin watched the dark. Maelis checked his traps. I sat by the fire, trying not to feel the thing I was trying not to feel.

Then I heard it.

Movement in the treeline. Low. Fast. Coming our way.

I shouted.

They came out of the dark. A pack of them. Lean. Long-limbed. Teeth and noise and hunger.

Maelis drew. The bowstring sang. One dropped.

Brannoc was up, axe in his hands. He swung. He connected. Another fell.

Wren moved. Low and fast, her knife finding flesh. In and out before the thing knew she was there.

Corwin was a wall. Blade out, stance wide, everything economy. He killed cleanly.

I used my voice. I dampened. I pushed. The pack faltered, confused.

We fought together. We held.

They broke. They turned. They ran.

We stood in the hollow, breathing hard. The fire flickered. The dark pressed in around us.

Brannoc clapped Maelis on the back. "You'll do, human."

Wren laughed. Bright. Easy. The kind of laugh that makes the night feel warmer.

Corwin looked at me. The door behind his eyes was open a crack — just enough. I nodded. He nodded back.

And Maelis looked at Wren. He looked at her the way I looked at Corwin. Like she was the sun, and he was standing in her light, and he knew it, and he would stand there as long as she let him.

We were five. I knew it would break our hearts before the song was done. But that was still to come. This was the good part, and the good part was worth the having.
