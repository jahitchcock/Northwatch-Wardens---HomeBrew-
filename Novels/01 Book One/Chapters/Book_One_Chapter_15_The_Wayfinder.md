# The Old Songs of Aevoria
## Book One — *She Who Would Not Be Silent*
### Chapter Fifteen — The Wayfinder

---

Thistlebrook was the end of the cultivated country. A frontier settlement in the way a wound is a frontier — a place where the world ended and the wild began, where the road stopped pretending and became a track, where the people stopped pretending to be safe and became brave.

We needed a guide. Someone who could lead us through the wild miles toward the Hollow Choir.

Her name was Wren.

---

I met her at the inn. She was sitting at a table near the window, a map spread in front of her, a cup of something steaming in her hand. She looked at the map the way I looked at a name-slip — reading not just lines but a language, a story. The story was the land, and the land was what she read the way I read memory.

She looked up when we came in. She saw Brannoc and smiled — warm, easy, the kind of smile that makes a stranger feel welcome. Brannoc smiled back. They were friends before they had spoken a word.

Then she looked at Corwin.

Something happened.

Corwin went still. Not the stillness of the grove. Not the stillness of the road. A different stillness — deeper, older. The stillness of a man seeing something he had seen before, many times, so many times that the seeing had become a kind of grief.

Then he went careful. Too careful. The kind of careful that is trying to hide what it feels.

Then he went warm. Warmer than I had ever seen him. Warmer than the fire. Warmer than anything.

Everyone called it a man unexpectedly smitten. Brannoc called it that. The innkeeper. The people at the other tables. They saw a man struck by a woman, and they called it ordinary.

But I saw what was not ordinary. This was not a man seeing a woman for the first time. This was a man seeing a woman he had seen before — many times, so many times that the seeing had become a kind of grief. I did not have a word for what he was. I filed it. Another impossibility.

---

Wren was a wayfinder and a healer. She read the land the way I read memory — could look at a hill and see the water under it, a track and see the animal that had made it, a sky and see the weather coming.

She was sharp. Warm. Fearless about things she should not be fearless about — and the fearlessness was what I admired and feared in equal measure, because it would make her brave, and the bravery would make her walk into the thing that would kill her.

She was funny. She laughed easily, spoke easily, moved easily. The kind of person who makes the world feel lighter, the road shorter, the night warmer.

I liked her. Immediately. The way you like a thing you know you will love.

---

Corwin argued against hiring her.

He argued hard, out of nowhere. "Too young. Too inexperienced. Too much of a thing we have no need of. We can find our own way. We have been finding our own way for weeks."

He stopped. He did not finish the sentence. I heard the thing he did not say — the thing he was arguing against was the thing he wanted.

Brannoc looked at him. "You're being strange, human. Stranger than usual. Stranger than the stone. And the strangeness tells me you're wrong. We're hiring her."

Corwin looked at Brannoc. Then at me. Then at Wren, who was standing at the door, who had heard everything.

She said, "I will be here at dawn. Packed. Ready. If you are here, we go. If you are not, I go alone. Either way, I go."

She left.

---

At dawn, she was there. Packed. Ready. And Corwin was there too.

Wren smiled. "You are the one who argued against hiring me."

"I was wrong," he said.

"I know. But you are here now. That is what matters."

We walked on.
