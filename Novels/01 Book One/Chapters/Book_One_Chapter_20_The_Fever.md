# The Old Songs of Aevoria
## Book One — *She Who Would Not Be Silent*
### Chapter Twenty — The Fever

---

An ordinary thing. The songs make our deaths grand and tragic, but this was not grand and not tragic. Just a fever. A wasting fever the road's remedies could not break.

Wren had walked out of monsters untouched. She had walked out of the wild miles unscathed. She had walked out of the thing that had run Corwin through, and she had not been touched.

And this was what took her. An ordinary fever. An ordinary failing of an ordinary body.

The ordinariness was the cruelty.

---

I felt the old helplessness return. The helplessness I had carried since Fenn — the child who could not save her brother, could not even mourn him aloud. I felt it the way you feel a wound that has not yet healed.

But this time I refused to be silent.

I spoke. I spoke about Fenn. I spoke about the helplessness, about the child I had been, about the brother I could not save and could not mourn aloud. The speaking made the feeling lighter.

Wren listened. She listened the way she always listened — fully, carefully, as though the hearing mattered.

"Elowen. I am sorry about your brother. I am sorry about the helplessness. I am sorry about the silence they made you carry."

I looked at her. She was dying, and she was comforting me.

But that is the rest of the song. This was still the fever, still the helplessness.

---

Corwin abandoned the Hollow Choir.

The songs make our quests into things pursued at all costs, but this was not that. He abandoned it without hesitation. The thing he had chased for ages, for centuries, for the whole of his endless life — and he abandoned it to stay at Wren's side.

The Hollow Choir was days away. He turned his back on it for her.

I watched him hold her hand and I understood the irony. He had chased the oldest song for longer than I could imagine. And he abandoned it to hold the living song dying in his arms.

The holding was more important than the finding. He had learned that, at least.

---

We stopped. We made camp. We did the things people do when someone they love is dying.

Wren was hot, then cold, then drifting. Corwin stayed at her side. He held her hand the way you hold a thing when you are afraid to let go. He spoke to her the way you speak to someone you love when they might not be there in the morning.

I watched. I kept. I waited.
