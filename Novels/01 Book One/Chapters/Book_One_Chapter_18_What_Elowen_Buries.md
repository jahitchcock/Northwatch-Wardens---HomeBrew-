# The Old Songs of Aevoria
## Book One — *She Who Would Not Be Silent*
### Chapter Eighteen — What Elowen Buries

---

I named it on a morning when the light was good. Not in the dark, not in the rain, not in the kind of weather that matches the feeling. A clear sky, an open road, good light.

I was walking beside Wren. She was talking about the land, the route, the things she was good at. The easiness of her talking made the walking easy. The easiness made the naming possible.

I looked at her. I understood something I had been carrying for a long time.

I loved him.

I loved Corwin. The man who had walked out of the mist and asked whether anyone still kept the old songs. The man who had looked at me across forty paces and seen a girl who was not silent. The man who had let me walk beside him, keep him, gather the small impossibilities.

I loved him. And the loving was hopeless. The hopelessness meant it should be buried. And burying was what I had been trained to do.

So I named it. And I buried it. In the same breath. In the same good light.

---

Wren was looking at me. "Elowen. You are not all right."

"I'm fine."

"You are not fine. You are thinking about something. You are carrying something."

I looked at her. I chose.

I chose her friendship. I chose her happiness. I chose Corwin's happiness. I chose what was good and right.

"I was thinking about what you asked me to keep," I said. "The small joy. The thing that is happening now. The good thing."

She smiled. "That is a good thing to think about. A good thing to keep."

I looked at her. Then at Corwin, walking ahead of us, his eyes on the horizon. I loved them both. I would carry the loving in the place where I kept the things that could not be spoken.

---

I gave it away. That is the truth I want to be honest about. The songs make our loves into things we keep and hold and bury. But I did not bury it. I gave it away.

I gave the feeling to Wren. To Corwin. To the road. To the sky. To the thing I could not keep.

I did not notice, at the time, that I had just practiced a lesson I could not yet articulate. I only noticed that the feeling was gone — given away, no longer mine.

---

We walked on. Wren talked. Corwin listened. Brannoc grumbled. Maelis watched. I walked beside them, part of the family that would break my heart before the song was done.

I loved him. I loved her. I loved them both. The loving was both a joy and a grief, and the both-ness of it was what made it worth the carrying.
