# The Old Songs of Aevoria
## Book One — *She Who Would Not Be Silent*
### Chapter Fourteen — What He Won't Say

---

He told us a story one night.

We were camped in a clearing — the kind the road makes when it passes through a wood and the wood gives way, reluctantly, to the sky. Brannoc had asked for it. "A story," he said, the way you ask for a story when the fire is good and the night is long.

Corwin looked at the fire. "Very well. I will tell you one."

He told it plainly. The way you tell a thing when you are telling it as folklore — as a thing you have heard, not a thing you have lived.

He told us about a man. A man who lived a long time. Longer than anyone should. Longer than anyone could. Longer than anyone would want to. The long living made him something not quite human, not quite anything — outside the world and looking in. The looking-in made him lonely. The loneliness made him love. The loving made him lose. The losing made him love again. The pattern went on and on, a wheel that did not stop. The man was both blessed and cursed by it.

Brannoc listened the way dwarves listen — with the particular attention of a people who believe stories should be heard well. When it was done, he clapped. "That's a good one, human. Sad, but good. The kind that stays with you."

Corwin looked at the fire. "Aye. It is a good story."

But I heard the catch in his voice. The way you hear a crack in a wall that is not yet breaking but will break. I understood, in that moment: the story was not folklore. It was not something he had heard. It was something he had lived. It was a confession disguised as a story, disguised as folklore, disguised as a thing that did not matter.

I did not say anything. I did not need to. The silence between us was the silence of two people who have heard a thing and understood it and are not yet ready to speak about it. The not-yet-ready-ness is the mercy. The mercy lets the walking go on.

---

Later, when Brannoc was asleep and the fire was low and the night was deep and the quiet made the world feel small and held, I heard Corwin humming.

The same tune. The same fragment. The same few notes he had hummed on the road three weeks ago, when we were still only the two of us.

He was humming it under his breath, the way you hum when your mind is elsewhere and your mouth is doing something your mind is not attending to.

Then he stopped.

The way you stop when you have touched something hot. The way you stop when you have said something you did not mean to say.

I said, "What."

He looked at me. The door was shut. "What tune."

"The one you were humming. Just now. The same one from three weeks ago. The same one you've been humming since I met you. What is it. Where does it come from. Why do you stop when you catch yourself."

He looked at the fire. "I do not know what it is. I do not know where it comes from. I do not know why I hum it. I do not know why I stop." He paused. "Let it go, Elowen. Sleep."

The deflection was less smooth this time. Less practiced. I noticed. But he was not ready. Not yet. Not that night.

I let it go. I filed it. Another impossibility added to the list.

---

In the dark, after he was asleep and the fire was out and the wood was quiet, I lay awake and I felt it.

The thing I had been trying not to feel. The thing I had been trying not to name. The thing I should not keep, should not feel, should not name — because naming would make it real, and real would mean I could not unfeel it.

But I felt it anyway.

I felt it the way you feel a thing in the body before the mind has named it — a spark in the dark, small and bright, the kind of thing that makes the heart go fast and the breath go short. Something was happening. Something was changing. Something was becoming.

I tried to bury it. I tried to file it away. The trying failed. The failing told me something: this was not a thing that could be buried.

I lay in the dark and I felt it and I did not bury it and I did not file it and I did not keep it. I let it be what it was. A spark. A small bright thing in the dark. Not yet a fire. Not yet a flame. Not yet a thing that would burn the world down. But a thing that was, in the dark, in the quiet, in the held-ness of the night, mine.
