## Summary

The Covenant of the Silent Vigil is a multi-faith religious order dedicated to protecting communities from supernatural threats, with a particular vigilance toward unregulated and unaccountable magic. Based in the holy city of Divinity's Beacon, the Covenant maintains temples, watchers, and divine casters across Aevoria — especially in regions where traditional authority is weak. Their combination of divine healing, military discipline, and ideological fervor makes them a natural ally for frontier guilds protecting the innocent, but their hard line against unchecked arcane power puts them in tension with scholars who believe dangerous knowledge must be studied, not suppressed.

## Leadership

- **High Warden Sera Lightbringer** (aasimar paladin) — Commander of the Covenant's military arm; drives the organization's interventionist posture and holds members to an unyielding standard of sacred duty.
- **Prior Merrik the Patient** (human cleric) — Oversees spiritual guidance and novice training; the pastoral counterweight to the High Warden's martial edge, though no less resolute in his convictions.

## Goals

1. Establish a permanent Watcher post in Northreach to monitor the escalating reports of strange magical phenomena — the frontier's weak authority structure alarms the Covenant's leadership.
2. Prevent any party — academic, commercial, or criminal — from weaponizing Aevorian magical research; if the Archivists of Aevor continue their work unchecked, the Covenant intends to intervene.

## Relationship to the Party

The Covenant views the Northwatch Wardens with cautious approval — frontier protectors who uphold community safety align with their mission. They will provide sanctuary, healing services on a sliding-scale donation basis, and useful intelligence about supernatural threats. The relationship is complicated by any Warden association with arcane scholars pursuing Aevorian research (which the Covenant considers dangerous) or by Wardens who shelter practitioners of pact magic without scrutiny.

## Season 1: Watchful Presence

Covenant clerics and traveling Watchers pass through Northreach settlements providing healing and gathering reports. They are not yet stationed in force on the frontier, but the anomalies being reported are attracting institutional attention. Introduce them as reliable sources of divine magic services and as NPCs who ask pointed questions about what the Wardens have encountered. High Warden Lightbringer communicates by letter; Prior Merrik may appear in person at Divinity's Beacon if the party travels south. Their stance toward the party depends heavily on how the Wardens handle Aevorian discoveries — sharing findings openly earns trust; concealing them breeds suspicion.

## Key Locations

- **Divinity's Beacon** — Covenant headquarters and holy city; center of multi-faith divine authority in Aevoria and the destination for major pilgrimage.
- **Covenant Waypost Temples (Northreach, Verdant Marches)** — Small but staffed; offer healing, sanctuary, and supernatural threat reporting.
- **Vharoxis (hostile absence)** — The Covenant considers Vharoxis hostile territory; they have no official presence and view the city as a symbol of what happens when accountability is abandoned.

## Secrets (DM Only)

- High Warden Sera Lightbringer has received internal reports from Covenant agents suggesting that Aevorian magical effects resist divine intervention in ways that haven't been seen since fragmentary accounts of the Great Fall. She is suppressing this information to prevent panic within the order — but she is quietly terrified that divine magic may be insufficient against the threat now manifesting in Northreach.
- Prior Merrik the Patient knows far more about Aevor's anti-divine weapons than he has shared publicly. During a crisis in his early ministry, he witnessed frigid woe kill people he could not save — divine healing simply failed. He blamed himself for years. He now recognizes Aevorian magical effects by how they resist healing and will react with visible panic if he encounters frigid woe again.
