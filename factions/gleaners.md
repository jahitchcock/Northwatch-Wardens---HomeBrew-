## Summary

The Gleaners are a community-focused cooperation network helping small farmers in the Verdant Marches share resources, negotiate fair prices collectively, and resist exploitation by large merchant guilds — particularly the Solace Trade Consortium. They are not a militant organization, but they are quietly politically effective: a region of farmers who collectively refuse to sell to a given buyer can reshape trade economics faster than any guild negotiation.

## Leadership

- **Harvest Speaker Noma Briarford** (halfling) — The Gleaners' most widely respected negotiator; coordinates collective bargaining and mediates disputes between member communities.

## Goals

1. Negotiate fair price agreements with the Solace Trade Consortium and independent merchants for the current harvest season.
2. Build enough food-sharing infrastructure between communities that a single bad harvest no longer threatens any member settlement with starvation.

## Relationship to the Party

The Gleaners have limited awareness of the Northwatch Wardens but positive associations with frontier guilds generally. If the party operates in the Verdant Marches or helps communities that the Gleaners serve, they will find cooperative locals, reliable food and supply access, and useful ground-level intelligence about what's moving through farming communities.

## Season 1: Background Resource

The Gleaners are primarily a regional flavor faction in Season 1. They become more relevant if the campaign's events affect agricultural communities or if the party needs community-level support structures in the Verdant Marches.

## Key Locations

- **Verdant Marches Farming Settlements** — The Gleaners' natural territory; the network is present in most small farming communities across the region.
- **Market Towns (seasonal)** — Gleaner representatives attend major seasonal markets to coordinate collective sales and monitor pricing.

## Secrets (DM Only)

- Harvest Speaker Noma Briarford has been receiving reports from Gleaner members about strangers purchasing specific types of grain and livestock in unusual quantities — not for food, but for something the buyers won't explain. The purchasing pattern matches the Aevorian Echo's interest in biological samples, but Noma has no context for that connection. She has the data; she is missing the framework to understand what it means.
