## Summary

The Freelance Brotherhood is less a formal organization than a reputation economy — a loose network of independent adventurers who share information, trade contract leads, and keep informal tabs on who can be trusted in a fight. Unlike the Northwatch Wardens, the Brotherhood has no central leadership, no charter, no code, and no headquarters; membership is claimed, not conferred. They operate wherever adventurers gather and are defined entirely by the standing they accumulate through completed work and honored agreements. Skilled when motivated, unreliable when not, and competitive by nature.

## Leadership

None. That is the point. Some members carry higher reputations than others, and well-regarded veterans may informally steer conversations or broker contract opportunities — but no one speaks for the Brotherhood as a whole, and anyone who tries will find the title evaporates instantly.

## Goals

1. Maintain the informal reputation network so that members can identify reliable collaborators and avoid dangerous contracts or untrustworthy clients.
2. Ensure the Brotherhood remains the default identity for adventurers who don't want the obligations of a formal guild — a permanent alternative to institutions like the Wardens.

## Relationship to the Party

The Brotherhood and the Northwatch Wardens exist in a relationship of professional competition with occasional overlap. Brotherhood members sometimes bid on the same contracts posted to the Waystone Board; others show up in frontier towns claiming capability they may or may not have. High-reputation Brotherhood adventurers will treat Wardens with collegial respect — competence is the only currency that matters. Low-reputation drifters may resent the Wardens' institutional credibility. The Brotherhood is not hostile, but it is a source of rival contractors, moral ambiguity, and useful information from people with no loyalty to share.

## Season 1: Background Noise

Brotherhood members appear as color NPCs in taverns, contract competitions, and settlement encounters. They provide a contrast to the Wardens' structured approach — some are impressive, some are reckless, a few are actively problematic. Use them to show what adventuring looks like without a code: capable but inconsistent. If the party builds reputation through Season 1 contracts, well-regarded Brotherhood members will seek them out for collaboration on jobs too large for a solo operator.

## Key Locations

- **Frontier Taverns and Markets** — The Brotherhood's natural habitat; information exchanges happen over meals and between contracts wherever adventurers gather.
- **Major City Gathering Spots** — In larger settlements, certain inns or guildhalls serve as informal Brotherhood contact points.
- **Contract Boards (shared)** — Brotherhood members post and respond to the same boards as the Wardens; the overlap creates both competition and opportunity.

## Secrets (DM Only)

- There is a high-reputation Brotherhood veteran named Cressida Holt operating in the Northreach frontier who is not as independent as she appears — she is a quiet intelligence asset for the Solace Trade Consortium, feeding information about frontier conditions (including Warden activity) back to Trade Prince Kallas Vermeer. She is genuinely skilled and not hostile, but she has an agenda that will eventually put her at odds with the party if the Consortium's interests diverge from the Wardens'.
